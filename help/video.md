# Video and cameras

Analog and digital TV, IP cameras, camera tools, podcasts and webcams.

## Analog video

Two blocks turn an IQ stream into a picture: **TV Demodulator** (IQ, worker) and **TV Decoder** (Decoders, worker; the picture is on the `img` output — show it with a *Frame* node).

- **TV Demodulator:** *FM* (FPV video transmitters on 5.8 GHz, analog satellite TV) — a discriminator with a fast arctangent; *AM (negative)* / *AM (positive)* — envelope detector for broadcast TV (tune to the vision carrier). Then a 4th-order Butterworth low-pass (*video bandwidth*, up to 0.45 × the sample rate). The output is a real stream at the input rate with the sync tips down; *invert* flips the sign. *Deviation* only scales the level.
- **TV Decoder:** input is a real stream at ≥ 8 MS/s (colour: ≥ 2.4 × the colour subcarrier — 10.6 MS/s PAL, 8.6 MS/s NTSC; HackRF at 12–20 MS/s, Airspy 10 MS/s).
  - *Sync:* a slicer between the sync tip and the blanking level (first from the minimum and maximum, then from the measured levels), sub-sample edge times, pulse widths tell a line sync (4.7 µs) from equalizing (2.35 µs) and field (27 µs) pulses. The line period is tracked (±3 %), a missing line is filled in, a lost lock is found again. The sync tip and the back porch of every line set the black level and the gain, so the picture does not need a level setting.
  - *Standard:* *auto* counts the lines between field syncs (≈ 305 → PAL 625, ≈ 253 → NTSC 525); or set PAL / NTSC by hand.
  - *Sync:* *free* needs no sync at all (weak signal, narrow bandwidth, a lost carrier): lines are drawn at the nominal line rate, the levels come from the statistics of each line, so even a trace of a picture shows. The picture slants or rolls until *trim* (ppm of the line period) matches the transmitter; *picture shift* moves it. No colour in this mode.
  - *Polarity:* *auto* flips it if there is no sync for half a second.
  - *Colour:* the burst of every line gives the phase and the gain of the subcarrier (the reference phase runs on across lines). PAL: the swinging burst gives the V-switch, the chroma is averaged with the previous line of the field. Luma = the composite minus the reconstructed chroma, so the subcarrier does not show on coloured areas; a weak chroma is treated as luma leakage. No burst (B/W) — luma only; *colour* off gives luma only too.
  - *Picture:* 384…960 px wide, 576 (PAL) or 480 (NTSC) lines; *weave* puts two fields in one frame, *bob* doubles the lines of every field (no combing on motion); brightness, contrast, picture shift in µs and lines.
  - Outputs: `img`, `lock` (0 / 1), `fps` (fields per second).
- **Not done:** SECAM (the picture comes out B/W), teletext and VBI lines, sound, VCR and other unstable line timing beyond ±3 %, blind PAL / NTSC colour of a signal whose subcarrier is off by several kHz. The field order (top or bottom first) is not detected, it follows the order of the syncs. Checked against the generator and hand-made signals, not against real cameras — the timing constants are the standard ones (PAL-B/G/I, NTSC-M without setup), check your own captures. At 12–20 MS/s the chain takes about one core: it runs in one worker island, lower the run speed if it does not keep up.

The IQ Generator mode **Analog TV** sends a test card (75 % colour bars, grey scale, white frame) in PAL or NTSC, FM (*deviation*) or AM with negative modulation.

Presets: *Analog TV: Test Card (Generator)*, *Analog TV: FPV / TV Receiver (USB SDR)*, *Analog TV: FPV Channel Scanner (HackRF)* (Band Scanner over the Raceband channels; the TV Decoder `lock` stops the scan on a live video, add rows to the table for more channels). *Analog TV: FPV Multiband Scanner (HackRF)* is the same scan over 21 channels: 0.9, 1.2, 2.4 GHz and Raceband. *Analog TV: FPV Mosaic (one HackRF)* shows several channels at once with the **TV Hopper**.

- **TV Hopper** (Decoders, worker): a mosaic of several analog channels from one receiver. Wire the receiver's `iq` to `in` and the hopper's `freq` to the receiver's `freq`: it tunes the receiver to each channel of *channels, MHz* (up to 16) in turn and takes one field (*bob*) on each, throws away chunks of the old frequency (the stream's `fc` must match) and puts the picture into the channel's tile (384×288, a grid on `img`). Every channel has its own demodulator and decoder whose state (polarity, sync levels, standard) is kept between visits, so a channel locks again within a field or two. *dwell* is the longest time on a channel without a finished field (a silent channel stays black), *settle* — how long to wait for the receiver to reach the channel. A tile refreshes every channels × (retune time + ~50 ms of signal for one field): the retune time of a real receiver decides, 4 channels ≈ 2–4 frames per second each. `hold` (input or parameter) = N stays on channel N and puts its full frame on `sel` at the full rate; `lock` — channels locked now, `ch` — the current channel. An empty channel is probed for a shorter *probe* time and visited less often. Checked against a simulated receiver (`node tools/test-tvhop.mjs`: two PAL channels and one noise channel, a field per visit takes ~50 ms of signal), not against real hardware

## Digital TV (MPEG-TS)

**TS → Video** (`tsvid`, Decoders): an MPEG transport stream on `bin` (or a `.ts` / `.m2ts` file chosen in the node) → PAT / PMT → the video and the audio of one program. The picture is on `vid` (a canvas: *Video Overlay*, *Record Video*, *WebRTC Video Out*), the sound (MP2, and AAC-ADTS through the browser's AudioDecoder) on `L` / `R` (to *Sound Card*); `lock` and `fps` are numbers. *program* picks the program number (0 — the first one with video), *buffer* is the delay before a frame is shown (frames go out by PTS), *audio delay* shifts the sound against the picture, *loop* repeats a file.

- **H.264, HEVC:** the browser's WebCodecs `VideoDecoder` (Annex B; the codec string comes from the SPS). A frame with a lost packet (continuity counter) is dropped; decoding goes on (a short artifact until the next I frame) — the `strict` option waits for the next entry point instead. The mode that worked (hardware / software, any I frame / IDR only) is remembered.
- **MPEG-2 video** (DVB-T SD, ATSC): browsers have no decoder, so `vendor/mpeg2.wasm` does it — 4:2:0, frame and field pictures, I / P / B, interlace (field prediction and field DCT), both scans, both coefficient tables, non-linear quantiser, DC precision up to 11 bits, custom matrices, 8 bit → RGBA (BT.601, BT.709 from 720 lines up), picture shaped by the display aspect ratio (4:3, 16:9). Interlaced pictures are shown as they are (no deinterlacing yet). Not supported: dual prime, scalability, 4:2:2, MPEG-1 video, `repeat_first_field` (3:2 pulldown is ignored).
- **MP2 audio** (MPEG-1 Layer II, what DVB-T carries): `vendor/mpeg2.wasm`, resampled to the engine rate, played by the video clock. AAC / AC-3 are named in the readout but not decoded yet.
- **Reception:** from a USB stick — *DVB USB (Astrometa)* below; a TS file works as well. **Not done:** showing teletext and subtitles (they are listed), encrypted programs, PTS wrap (every 26.5 h).
- **Channel list and what else the stream carries:** the service tables are parsed while it plays — SDT (names, provider, type, scrambled), NIT (network name, logical channel numbers, mux frequencies), PMT (every stream with its language: audio tracks, subtitles, teletext), EIT (now / next and the schedule), TDT / TOT (UTC and local offset), plus the bitrate of each program. Outputs: `all` (both kinds in one list; better keep a Table per kind: `chan`, `epg`), `chan` (one row per service: lcn, program, name, type, scrambled, video, audio, extra, mbit, now, until, next, provider, playing) and `epg` (one row per event: channel, start in local time, min, title, text, state) — wire one of them to the `rows` input of a **Table** (rows in: replace). `info` is a text with the network, the time and the number of services. The `prog` input takes a program number (e.g. from a Table column) and selects it. Tables arrive within a few seconds (EIT schedule within about 10 s).
- **For a Table without flicker:** set the Table's *rows wire* (fields panel → List) to **sync** (the upsert key column, `id` by default: rows are changed, added and removed in place — nothing is cleared, the selected row stays) or **new only** (adds the rows whose key is not in the list yet, e.g. into a band plan). Every row from `all` / `chan` / `epg` / `mux` has an `id`. A Table column wired to `prog` switches the channel (a number, or a part of the channel name).
- **Multiplex frequencies:** `mux` (TS → Video: the muxes the NIT announces, DVB-T and DVB-T2; DVB USB: the muxes the stick locked on, also from a scan) are band-plan rows in kHz (`name`, `lo`, `hi`, `step`, `color`, `kind`, `note`). The `freq` input of DVB USB takes MHz, kHz or Hz (by size), so the `mid` output of such a band plan tunes the stick.
- **Teletext:** the teletext stream of the program (DVB, EN 300 472) is decoded into pages (EN 300 706 level 1: colours, block graphics, double height; Cyrillic G0 for Russian, Ukrainian, Bulgarian streams or by the `characters` option). *node window shows → teletext* puts the page into the node window; the `ttx` output is the picture, `ttxt` the text, the `page` input and the ◀ ▶ buttons choose the page. Not done: fastext links, X/26 accents, hold mosaic, flash.
- **Preset:** *Digital TV: DVB-T2 full* — the stick, TS → Video, three Tables — channels (click a row to switch), programme, muxes (band plan; its mid output tunes the stick).
- **Build:** `tools/mpeg2/build.sh` (clang, wasm32, no libc; 41 kB). The video decoder (`mpeg2.c`) is new code; its macroblock syntax and VLC trees follow [pl_mpeg](https://github.com/phoboslab/pl_mpeg) (MIT, Dominic Szablewski), the run / level tables are the standard's Annex B tables as listed in FFmpeg's `mpeg12data.c`. The MP2 decoder (`mp2.c`) is pl_mpeg's audio decoder (kjmp2 by Martin J. Fiedler) with two corrections: its requantisation scale was 1 / (levels + 1) short of the standard's table, which made coarse quantisers up to 25 % too quiet, and the output divisor compensated for it by 1.5 %.
- **Tests:** `node tools/test-ts.mjs` (demuxer: PAT / PMT / PES, SPS size and cropping, chunked input with junk, a lost packet); `node tools/test-mpeg2.mjs` (needs ffmpeg: streams made by ffmpeg's `mpeg2video` / `mp2` encoders are decoded by ffmpeg and by the wasm module and compared — progressive, intra only, P only, interlaced 720×576 with field MC and field DCT, alternate scan, intra VLC table B.15, non-linear quantiser, DC precision 10 bit, custom matrices, 1920×1080; the pictures agree to 60–64 dB PSNR, max difference 5 levels, the IDCT rounding; audio −73 dB). 300 corrupted streams ran without a trap. **Not tested:** field pictures (no encoder here makes them), dual prime (not implemented), real DVB captures; the H.264 / HEVC path was not run in a browser with a decoder yet.

### DVB USB (Astrometa)

**IPTV** (`iptv`, Sources): a TV stream from the network as `bin` for **TS → Video**. The URL (parameter, or the `url` text input) can be an HTTP MPEG-TS stream, an **HLS** playlist (a media playlist with `.ts` segments, or a master playlist — *quality* picks the variant: highest / lowest / first; live streams start at the live edge, a VOD plays at the segment pace) or an **M3U channel list** (`#EXTINF`, `tvg-id`, `tvg-logo`, `group-title`): the channels go out on `rows` (wire to a *Table*; a row's `url` column to the `url` input switches the channel) and *channel* / the `channel` input plays one — a number (1-based; the `n` column of the rows), a `tvg-id` / name, or a ready `http(s)://` address, so a Table column (`n`, `id`, `name` or `url`) can be wired straight to it; the `next` pulse input and the ◀ ▶ buttons step through the list; a list can also be loaded from a file. Outputs `name`, `live`, `kbps`. The server must allow cross-origin reads (CORS) — otherwise tick *through the CORS proxy* (Settings); an `http://` stream from an `https://` page is blocked by the browser (mixed content). **Not possible in a browser:** UDP / RTP multicast (`udp://@239…`), RTSP, RTMP; **not supported:** fMP4 / CMAF HLS and encrypted (AES-128) HLS — the node says so. Dropped streams reconnect. Preset *Digital TV: IPTV (HTTP TS / HLS / M3U) → TS → Video and Sound*. Logic: `modules/iptv-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-iptv.mjs`; the network side is not checked against a real server.

**Video Player** (`vplayer`, Sources; `modules/vplayer.js`, logic in `modules/vplayer-kernels.js`, no DOM; `node tools/test-vplayer.mjs`): a player for a list of video sources kept in a **Table** (`name`, `url` columns; wire `all` / `rows` to `rows`, columns `link` / `src` / `title` also work). A source is a file (mp4 / webm / ogg) or an **HLS** playlist (`.m3u8`: Safari and new Chrome play it themselves, other browsers through `vendor/hls.min.js` (hls.js 1.5, Apache-2.0), cached for offline use and loaded on the first HLS; the quality list appears for a master playlist); DASH is not supported. **Seek**: the slider, a **double tap** on the left / right third of the picture (−10 / +10 s, repeated taps add up; the middle — fullscreen), keys ← → (Shift — 30 s), Space, F (fullscreen), PageUp / PageDown (source), the `seek` input (seconds), speed 0.5–2×. The source is picked in the list under the picture, with ⏮ ⏭, with the `next` pulse or with `channel` (a number from 1, a name or a ready address — a Table column wires straight in). **Series**: columns `series` (or `show`), `season`, `episode` — or the numbers found in the name (`S01E02`, `1x02`, `Episode 3`, `3 серия`, `Сезон 2`); the list is ordered by series, season and episode and grouped by season in the picker (`S01E02 · name`), a source marked ✓ is watched, ▸42 % is started; at the end of an episode the next one starts (*at the end go on to the next source*), **↻ Continue watching** (also a button of the node) opens the last started source from its position, and after a finished episode the next one. **Saved position**: the position of every address is kept in the browser (the last 300; every 5 s, on pause, seek, source change and page close) and the same address continues from it, also after a reload; the start (< 5 s) and the end (≥ 95 %) begin from zero; *Forget saved positions* clears it. Every save also goes out on `rec` as `{url, name, series, season, episode, pos, dur, pct, done, t}` — wire it to a Table with key `url` and it becomes a watch history (survives a reload, is backed up and synced). **Modes**: the header has the same buttons as the picture modules — **⛶ expand** (the parameters hide, the picture and the controls fill the module), **⤢ fullscreen** (the picture *with* the control bar: seek, source list, speed; double tap the edges to jump — also in the fallback mode of browsers without the Fullscreen API), **⧉ picture in picture** (the system PiP window of the video itself, with sound; its play / pause / ±10 s / previous / next buttons and the lock-screen and headset keys come from the Media Session API) and, in the DSP app for Android, **🗔 float over other apps** and PiP of the app (frames through a canvas — only for a server that allows CORS for the video; there the window has no seek buttons, use the player). The same buttons are on the dashboard tile. **A page of Internet Archive** (`https://archive.org/details/ID`) pasted into *URL* / `url` is not a video: the player reads the files of the item and plays them as a series. **A file from the device**: the **📂** button of the player (or dropping the file on the picture) opens a `.torrent` file — it goes to the torrent client of the app, so there is no need for a link to a tracker that wants a login — or a video / audio file, which the browser plays by itself (a `blob:` address of that tab). **A page of PeerTube** (`https://server/videos/watch/ID`, `/w/ID`, `/videos/embed/ID`) is not a video either: the player asks the server's API (`/api/v1/videos/ID`) and plays the HLS playlist, with the mp4 files of the other qualities as the fallback (the `blob:` address that the page's own player shows exists only in that tab and cannot be played elsewhere). Preset *Video: PeerTube Page Link → Player*. If a file does not play, the player tries the other formats of the same video (the item's h.264 derivative is preferred to the original, which may be DivX / MPEG-4 Part 2 or an unsupported container) and then says why (`MEDIA_ERR_SRC_NOT_SUPPORTED`, network…) and which address. **libVLC (DSP app for Android) is the converter, the player is the page's own `<video>`.** libVLC 3.6.0 (**LGPL 2.1** — linked dynamically, its own `.so` files in the APK; source: videolan.org) only *opens* an address and repacks it into **HLS**: the segments (mpegts, `livehttp`, a sliding window of 40 × 2 s) go to the app cache and a loopback server (`HlsRelay` in `VlcStream.kt`, `127.0.0.1`, random path, CORS) gives them to the ordinary `<video>` + hls.js of the page. There is no native player in the app (ExoPlayer, the full-screen overlay and the floating libVLC window were removed): seek, speed, Media Session, PiP and fullscreen are those of the browser. It opens almost any address — mkv / avi / DivX, AC3 / DTS, multicast **`udp://@239.1.1.1:1234`** and **`rtp://@239.1.1.1:5004`** (a Wi-Fi multicast lock is taken, the phone must be in the provider's network), `rtsp://`, `rtmp://`, `mms://`, **`smb://server/share/film.mkv`** (`smb://user:password@server/…`), `ftp://`, `sftp://`, `nfs://`, `upnp://` — put it into a Table like any other source (also from an M3U list: *Video Sources → M3U list*). *Who plays* (`engine`): *auto* — the browser, and in the app libVLC for the addresses and containers a browser does not know (smb / ftp / sftp / nfs / upnp / rtmp / mms, udp / rtp / rtsp, mkv / ts / avi / mpg / flv / wmv) and once for a file the browser failed to play; *browser* — never libVLC; *vlc stream* — any address through libVLC (older values *native (app)* / *vlc* mean this too). Bridge: `vlc.stream.open {url, start (ms), mode, audio, sub, burn, deint, hevc, adelay, vkbps, width, abr, rate, caching, seg}` → `{sid, url, tracks, dur, title, plan}` and `vlc.stream.close {sid}` (2 streams at most; events `{src:"vlc", ev:"stream", sid, state}`). *mode* `auto`: H.264 and AAC / MP3 pass **as they are** (remux — almost no load), and so does **HEVC** when the page's `<video>` can decode it (the page checks `MediaSource.isTypeSupported` for `hvc1` and sends `hevc:true`; the WebView decodes it on the hardware, the phone does nothing but repack); the rest (DivX, MPEG-2, VP9, AC3, DTS, FLAC… or an unknown codec) is re-encoded to H.264 (x264 `veryfast`) / AAC; `remux` never re-encodes, `transcode` always does; deinterlace and burnt-in subtitles force the video to be re-encoded. The tracks of the file come back in the reply: under the picture appear the **audio-track** list (if there are several) and the **subtitle** list (burnt into the picture, or off); a change, like a seek outside the written window, starts the stream again from the current second (`:start-time`, `:audio-track`, `:sub-track`, `:audio-desync`). Inside the window a seek is the usual `<video>` seek. Position, duration, saved positions, history and series work as usual (`vpT` / `vpD` add the start offset). Parameters of the player: *libVLC stream* mode, bitrate, width, deinterlace, burn subtitles, audio delay, cache. **From another computer** (a laptop watches a film that the phone opens): point the player's *bridge* at the phone (`ws://phone-address:8765`, a token from the app's settings, or a device name from Settings → Devices) — `vlc.stream.*` is allowed to a LAN client with the bridge token when the app has *share on LAN* and *open commands* on (the other `vlc.*` commands stay for the app's own page); the relay then listens on all interfaces and the stream address carries the phone's LAN address you connected to (random path, CORS). The stream is plain `http://`, so the laptop page must be `http://` or `localhost` (an `https://` page blocks it as mixed content); the re-encoding is done by the phone — prefer `remux` for H.264 / AAC files. The APK grows by ~25 MB per architecture (arm64 + arm). Checked here: the sout lines, the re-encode plan by codecs, the safe cache path, the relay (CORS, token, intact segments) on a JVM against the real libVLC classes (`gradle -p tools/android/jvm-test runVlcTest`), the page logic (`node tools/test-vplayer.mjs`) and the page against a stub bridge with a real HLS in Chromium; **not** libVLC on a phone: whether the x264 encoder and the `livehttp` output are in the libVLC build of the APK, whether libVLC writes `#EXT-X-ENDLIST` at the end of a file (otherwise the end is not detected by the player), whether hls.js (or native HLS) plays HEVC in mpegts on the device, the load of re-encoding (x264 is software; libVLC decodes through MediaCodec, `:avcodec-hw=any`, but has no hardware encoder), multicast and the start delay (a few seconds) are open. Presets *Video: Network Folders (SMB / FTP / UPnP) and Any Format → libVLC (DSP app)*, *Video: libVLC Stream into the Player — Audio Tracks, Subtitles, Seek, Double Tap (DSP app)*.

**Fullscreen and PiP in the DSP app for Android**: the **fullscreen of the device** (⤢, double tap the middle of the picture, F): the app implements `onShowCustomView`, so the Fullscreen API works in the WebView (system bars hidden, Back leaves it); where it does not, the player moves to the page body, and the node on the canvas keeps its height meanwhile. A server that does not support seeking (no `Range`), a failure in the middle of playback (two retries from the same second, then the other formats, then libVLC) and the code of the error are reported in the player's status. **⧉ picture in picture** is the system PiP window of the video itself (with sound, Media Session buttons); **🗔 float over other apps** and the PiP of the app get frames through a canvas (a server without CORS gives a note with the time instead of an empty picture — tick *allow picture capture*, the server must then send CORS; a libVLC stream is served by the app with CORS, so it always works). In the dashboard the player's tile has three buttons: **fullscreen** (the whole device screen), **PiP** and **Canvas only** («expand canvas»: the parameters hide, the picture and the seek bar fill the tile — inside the module, not on the whole screen).
**Torrents (DSP app for Android, through the bridge)**: a source `magnet:?xt=urn:btih:…` or the address of a `.torrent` file (a Table row, the `url` input, or *Video Sources → Samples* — the open Blender films; for an Internet Archive item tick *torrent* in *Video Sources*). The app (`TorrentHub.kt`, libtorrent4j; bridge commands `torrent.add` / `torrent.stream` / `torrent.status` / `torrent.list` / `torrent.remove` / `torrent.stop`, only for the page of the app) finds the peers (DHT, trackers of the link), downloads **only the chosen file, the pieces near the playing place first**, and serves it to the player on `http://127.0.0.1:<port>/t/<token>/…` with `Range` — so the file is an ordinary source: seek, saved position, history, picture-in-picture; mkv / avi / ts go through libVLC (HLS for the same `<video>`), mp4 / webm are played by the page directly. A torrent with several videos becomes a list of episodes (`…#file=N` is the stable key of a file; names `S01E02`, `3 серия` give seasons and episodes), *Continue* and auto-advance work. The status line shows `torrent: 37 % of the torrent · ↓ 1.2 MB/s · 14 peers`; with no peers it adds the DHT node count and the state of every tracker (`trackers: opentrackr: timed out; explodie: ok`) — so it is visible whether UDP gets through. **Only a window is downloaded, not the whole file**: the app asks for the pieces from the playing place on (≈ 96 MiB ahead, high priority) and for the head and the tail of the file (the header and the index of mp4 / mkv); the rest waits until the player gets there or seeks, other files of the torrent are not touched. The connections to peers are kept (libtorrent closes «redundant» ones by default, and a torrent with nothing wanted is «finished» and drops the seeds — then the peers had to be found again: «0 peers»); until a file is picked the torrent is in upload mode and downloads nothing. With no peers the status line also shows the last messages of libtorrent (`log: tracker_error … Host not found`, `listen_failed …`, `dht_error …`), so it is visible what exactly fails. A magnet is added to the session at once and stays in it (only the metadata is fetched at first — the file is not downloaded until the player picks it), so the peers found during the metadata search are not lost; the local address answers `503` until the first pieces arrive and the player retries (up to 30 × 3 s) instead of reporting «format not supported»; the search that timed out keeps going in the background — press again to wait further. *bridge* sets the app (`ws://127.0.0.1:8765` or `@device` — another phone with the app); the metadata search waits up to 90 s and shows how it goes in the status line (`looking for the metadata — 35 s · DHT nodes: 87 · peers: 2`; «DHT is silent» means UDP is blocked or there is no network, «no peers yet» — a rare torrent); a magnet with fewer than three trackers gets open public trackers added (many links carry only DHT). An `http(s)` address that is not a video (e.g. `…/dl.php?t=123`) is tried as a torrent once, then the player says that it is neither; trackers that need a login do not give the file by a link — the **open a .torrent file** button of the node reads it from the phone, or use the magnet link. The downloaded files live in the app's own folder and are deleted when its service stops or by `torrent.remove`; no seeding policy beyond what libtorrent does while the file is open. **From another computer:** the player's *bridge* may point at the phone (`@name` from Settings → Devices): the torrent commands are allowed to a LAN client with the bridge token when *open commands* are on in the app, and the file is served on the phone's LAN address (needs *share on LAN*) — the same stream address as for the libVLC stream, `http://` only (an `https://` page blocks it). Without the app a `magnet:` link says that it needs the app. Only content you may watch: the samples and Internet Archive items are open; no search over torrent sites is built in. Checked here: the pure logic and the real libtorrent (a seeder and a downloader on 127.0.0.1, the file read over HTTP with ranges in the middle, at the end, whole, 416, wrong token — `gradle -p tools/android/jvm-test runTorrentTest`) and the page against a stub of the bridge; **not** on a phone, with real trackers / DHT or with Android's network policies. Preset *Video: Torrents (magnet / .torrent) → Player (DSP app)*.
Outputs `vid` (the `<video>` element — to Frame, motion detectors, object detection…; empty until the first frame), `rec`, `name`, `time`, `dur`, `pct`, `playing`, `end`. A server must allow cross-origin reads for HLS (CORS); an `http://` link on an `https://` page is blocked by the browser. Presets *Video: Table of Sources → Player with Seek and Saved Position*, *Video: Internet Archive Search → Episodes → Player*, *Video: IPTV Channels by Country (iptv-org) → Player*.

**VLC Source** (`vlcsrc`, Sources; `modules/vlcsrc.js`, logic in `modules/vlcsrc-kernels.js`, no DOM; `node tools/test-vlcsrc.mjs`; the app side `VlcHub.kt`, bridge commands `vlc.open` / `vlc.close` / `vlc.cmd` / `vlc.list`, `gradle -p tools/android/jvm-test runVlcTest`): the sound of **any address** as a graph signal, through libVLC of the DSP app for Android. *url*: a file or a stream on the network (http / https / HLS), multicast `udp://@239.1.1.1:1234` or `rtp://@…`, `rtsp://`, `rtmp://`, `smb://`, `ftp://`, `sftp://`, `nfs://`, `upnp://` — AC3 / DTS / FLAC / AAC / Opus and the codecs a browser does not know (`file://`, `fd://`, `content://` are refused: files of the phone stay closed). libVLC decodes and sends **raw PCM s16le by UDP to 127.0.0.1:port** (`#transcode{acodec=s16l,channels,samplerate}:std{access=udp,mux=raw}`); the page listens to the port with the bridge command `udp.open` (a random port 40000–59999), assembles frames from the datagrams (a tail that is not a whole frame goes on into the next one) and gives them to the graph like the microphone of the phone: samples with time marks, interpolated to the rate of the graph by the clock of the engine blocks. Outputs `l`, `r` (signals; mono — both the same), `title`, `pos`, `dur` (s), `playing`, `end` (a pulse); inputs `url`, `go` (restart), `pause` (level). *rate* — the rate asked from libVLC (`graph` — the graph's), *channels*, *caching* — libVLC's network cache, *lag* — the smoothing reserve in the graph (less — shorter delay, but gaps if the pieces come unevenly), *bridge* — `ws://127.0.0.1:8765` or `@device`. Up to 4 sources at a time; the node stops its source when it is removed or the app's service stops. The delay is hundreds of milliseconds (the cache of libVLC + the reserve) — not for work where the delay matters; the file is expected to be paced in real time by libVLC (a source that delivers faster than real time makes the clock restart and gives gaps). Checked here: the PCM assembly, the `sout` line and the address filter on a JVM (Kotlin compiles against the real libVLC classes), the node against a stub of the bridge (a tone → the right signal on both outputs, pause, stop, an error without the bridge); **not** libVLC on a phone, real streams or the delay. Preset *VLC Source: Any Sound (Network Folder, Stream, Multicast) → Spectrum and Speaker (DSP app)*. Next: the video of a source (MJPEG into IP Camera) and *VLC Out* (the graph's sound out through libVLC: Icecast, RTP, a file, Chromecast). **Picture**: with *video* = «MJPEG → pin «video»» libVLC takes the picture of the same address too (a second connection to it, so a server that allows one viewer only gives one of the two), encodes it to MJPEG (*vfps*, *vwidth*, *vkbps*) and the app serves it on `127.0.0.1` with a random path and CORS headers; the address comes out on the *video* pin — wire it to the *url* of **IP Camera** (it gives the picture to Frame, Camera Archive, object detection…). Bridge commands `vlc.video.open` / `vlc.video.close`. Preset *VLC Source: Picture and Sound of Any Address…*.

**VLC Out** (`vlcout`, Output; `modules/vlcout.js`, logic in `modules/vlcout-kernels.js`, no DOM; `node tools/test-vlcout.mjs`; the app side `VlcHub.kt`, bridge commands `vlc.out.open` / `vlc.out.close` / `vlc.out.renderers`, `gradle -p tools/android/jvm-test runVlcTest`): the sound of the graph **out** of the DSP app for Android through libVLC — the opposite of VLC Source. **Stream Check** (`streamCheck`, Data): checks a channel list before you play it. Wire *IPTV* `rows` (any rows with a `url` column) to `rows`: the node requests every distinct address in *parallel checks* threads (1–64) and sends the working channels on `rows` (the fields are kept, plus `alive`, `check` — `TS` / `HLS` / `playlist` / `reachable` / the content type — and `ms`) and the rest on `dead` (with `why`: `HTTP 404`, `timeout`, `not a stream (text/html)`, …); `ok` / `bad` / `left` are counts. *check*: `content` reads the first *bytes* of the answer (HTTP status, the MPEG-TS sync byte 0x47 at 0 and 188, an M3U / HLS playlist, an audio / video content type) and needs CORS on the server; `reachable` only learns whether the server answers (works without CORS, the content is not checked); `auto` (default) reads the content and falls back to *reachable* where the browser forbids reading. *HLS: also fetch a segment* goes through a master playlist to the last segment of a media playlist and checks it is MPEG-TS (fMP4 / encrypted playlists are reported as such). *timeout* is per stream. Results are kept while the node lives, so changing the list only checks the new addresses; *Check again* forgets them, *Stop* cancels the run. The output list grows as the checks finish (at most about once a second): in the *Table* set *rows wire* = `sync`, so the list is updated in place and the selected row stays. An `http://` stream from an `https://` page is blocked by the browser and shows as dead; *through the CORS proxy* applies the Settings proxy. A stream is checked for the first bytes only, not for being watchable. Preset *Digital TV: IPTV* has it between IPTV and the Table. Logic: `modules/streamcheck-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-streamcheck.mjs` (a local HTTP server: TS, 404, HTML, a stalled stream, HLS with a good and a missing segment, the thread limit); the browser side is not checked against real IPTV servers.

### IP cameras and archive

Camera links live in a **Table** (`name`, `url` columns), the picture is loaded by a module over HTTP and goes on to the video modules — the same chain as IPTV.

**IP Camera** (`ipcam`, Sources): `url` is an **MJPEG stream** (multipart, the frames are cut out of it), a **JPEG / PNG snapshot** fetched every *snapshot: refresh every* seconds, or an **HLS** playlist (goes out on `url` — wire it to **IPTV**). *kind* `auto` decides by the address and, failing that, by the server's `Content-Type`. `user:password@host` in the address is sent as Basic auth. The address comes from the *url* parameter or input, from a Table row on `rec`, or from a list on `rows` (Table `all`) and the camera number / name in *cam* (the `cam` input, `next` pulse steps through the list) — so any number of IP Camera nodes share one Table, each with its own number. Outputs `img` (up to *max width*), `url` (HLS), `name`, `new`, `fps`, `live`. A stalled stream is reopened after 15 s, a dropped one reconnects. The camera must send CORS headers, otherwise tick *through the CORS proxy* (Settings); an `http://` camera from an `https://` page is blocked by the browser. **Not possible in a browser:** RTSP / RTMP / UDP — the node says so (a gateway such as go2rtc turns them into MJPEG / HLS).

**Camera Archive** (`camarch`, Video): *record* — frames from `img` go to the browser storage (Files → images → `Cameras/<name>`) every *a frame every* seconds, and also when the picture changes by more than *motion* (a 32×24 grey preview is compared; at most once a second); the `rec` input (≥ 0.5) is a gate. *keep days* / *keep at most frames* drop the oldest. *playback* — the archive of the camera named in *cam* (or on the `name` input) is shown with a **timeline** (frame density, the cursor): click the strip, the *position* slider, ◀ ▶, `seek` (0…1), `step` and `play` inputs, *playback: frames per second*, *loop*; `img` is the shown frame, `t` / `time` its time, `count` the frames. *Download this frame* saves a JPEG; the files are ordinary files in the Files manager (also exported with the backup).

**Fullscreen:** the ⤢ button in the module header (or in the tile header) and a double-click on the picture open the picture of **IP Camera**, **Camera Archive**, **Frame** and **TS → Video** (IPTV, DVB) fullscreen with the aspect ratio kept; Esc or another double-click returns. Where the browser cannot fullscreen a canvas (iOS), the picture fills the window instead. In fullscreen the archive answers ←, →, space. A module opts in with `fs:true` in its definition.

Preset *Cameras: Table of links → IP Camera → Frames + Archive*. Logic: `modules/camera-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-camera.mjs` (address kinds, auth, camera choice, motion, save schedule, pruning, timeline). The browser side was run against a mocked camera (MJPEG, snapshot, archive, playback, fullscreen), **not against real cameras**.

### Camera tools

Four *Video* nodes take the picture from `img` (*Camera*, *IP Camera*, *Video*…); *Timelapse* also takes `vid`. Logic — `modules/camtools-kernels.js`, nodes — `modules/camtools.js`, `node tools/test-camtools.mjs` (motion, pulse of a synthetic signal with noise and a drift, timelapse schedule and deflicker, ZIP checked by Python, colour temperature, lines and calibration of a spectrum). The browser side was run on the fake camera of headless Chromium, **not on real cameras or fingers**.

- **Motion Detect**: the share of pixels that changed by more than *pixel change that counts*, in the whole frame or a zone. Outputs `motion` (0…1), `go` (a pulse when the motion starts), `on` (a level, kept for *keep on after the motion stops* seconds; it needs the share above *alarm: % of the zone*), `level`, `cx` / `cy` (where the motion is), `img` (the map of changes) and `count`. *Compare with*: the previous frame (quick motion) or a slowly updated background (slow motion and left objects; a still object fades into the background). The `thr` input sets the alarm level, `reset` clears the background. `go` / `on` can start *Camera Archive* (`rec`), *Timelapse* (`snap`), *Beep*, *Camera → Record video* (`rec`).
- **Pulse (camera)**: heart rate from the colour of the picture. Cover the lens of the phone with a fingertip and turn *Camera → torch* on — or show a face (green channel, *only a zone*: forehead, cheeks). The mean colour of the zone over time → a band 0.15–1 s → a spectrum → `bpm`; `conf` (0…1) is the share of power at the peak and its second harmonic, below *trust above this confidence* the `ok` output is 0 and `bpm` is 0. `beat` — a pulse per heartbeat (about 0.4 s late, the filter looks ahead), `hrv` — RMSSD in ms, `finger` — the lens is covered. Needs ≥ 12 frames per second and a steady hand; it is not a medical device.
- **Timelapse**: a frame every *a frame every* seconds from `vid` (full resolution, up to *frame width*) or `img`, JPEG in memory (the page's memory: **frames are lost when the tab closes** — download them), up to *stop after frames*. Start / stop by the button or the `rec` level, `snap` — one more frame by a pulse, `interval` input. Outputs `count`, `go` (a pulse per frame), `on`, `left` (seconds to the next frame), `mb`. **Render video…** plays the frames at *frames per second* into webm / mp4 (takes as long as the video itself); *even out flicker* levels the brightness of neighbouring frames, a slow change (sunset) stays. **Download frames (zip)** — the JPEG files without re-encoding. For a long watch with a disk archive and a timeline use *Camera Archive* (a frame every N seconds or on motion).
- **Color / Spectrometer**: *color* — the mean colour of a zone: `r`, `g`, `b`, `hex`, `name`, `h` / `s` / `v`, `cct` (colour temperature, K; a white screen is about 6500 K). *spectrum* — a camera behind a diffraction grating (a CD piece or a grating film in front of the lens, a slit in front of the light): a strip of the frame across the rainbow becomes a spectrum by wavelength. Outputs `nm` (the strongest line), `lines` (the lines, nm) and `profile` (a signal for the scope). Calibration is a straight line through two points (*position in the strip* ↔ *nm*); **Calibrate on a fluorescent lamp** fits it to the three brightest lines of the lamp (Hg 436.6, Hg 546.1, Eu 611.6 nm). *Take dark frame* (lens covered) is subtracted, *hold maximum* collects the spectrum over time. The accuracy is a few nm at best, set by the grating and the sensor; a phone's auto exposure and white balance are better off (*Camera → auto exposure / auto white balance* off).

- **Screen (video)** (Sources): the picture of a screen, a window or a browser tab (`getDisplayMedia`; the browser asks what to share) on `vid` (for *Record Video*, *Files*, *Timelapse*, *Video Overlay*, *Object Detect*) and `img` (for *Color / Spectrometer*, *Motion Detect*, *Pulse*). **A live pixel**: click or drag over the preview — the circle marks a pixel (or the average of a square around it, *± px*) that is read in the full resolution of the capture, not from the small `img`; `r` `g` `b` `hex` `name` follow it while the screen changes; the `x` / `y` inputs move the point. The **Eyedropper** button is the browser's `EyeDropper` (Chrome / Edge, desktop): one pixel anywhere on the screen, even outside the browser window — but only once, the API has no live colour; switch *colour outputs from* back to *screen pixel* for a live one. What the capture shows depends on the browser (a window that is minimised stops updating; a browser tab is the most reliable). Preset *Screen: Live Pixel Color + Video Recording*.

Presets (*Images & TV*): *Pulse: Fingertip on the Phone Camera (torch)*, *Camera Alarm: Motion → Archive + Beep*, *Timelapse: Camera Frames → Video*, *Color Meter and Spectrometer (camera + grating)*, *Screen: Live Pixel Color + Video Recording*.

### Podcasts, Webcams, Free Music

Three network sources in the manner of *Internet Radio*: a search fills a list (`rows`), a pick gives an address (`url`, `name`) for **Audio Stream (URL)**, **Playlist** or **IPTV**. No keys or accounts. A browser needs the server to send CORS headers: where it does not, tick *through the CORS proxy* (the proxy from Settings; it wraps the search, the feed and the `url` output). An `http://` file from an `https://` page is blocked by the browser (mixed content).

**Podcasts** (`podcasts`, Sources): *query* searches Apple's public podcast directory (iTunes Search API; *store country* sets the catalogue) and the found shows go out on `rows` (`url` is the RSS feed). *podcast* takes a name from the results or any RSS feed URL (also the `feed` text input, e.g. a Table column): the feed is parsed (title, publication date, `itunes:duration`, the audio `enclosure`) and the episodes go out on `episodes`, the newest first (*episodes kept from a feed* limits their number). *episode* (the number, 1 is the newest; the `episode` input also takes a title part or an address), ◀ ▶ and the `next` pulse choose one: `url` is its audio file, `name` its title. Podcast hosts often have no CORS headers and redirect through trackers — the proxy is usually needed for the audio.

**Webcams** (`webcams`, Sources): *Find webcams* asks OpenStreetMap (Overpass, the same mirrors as the map's object layers) for objects with `contact:webcam` and for `man_made=surveillance` + `surveillance:type=webcam` within *radius* of the centre — the `lat` / `lon` wires, then the *centre* parameters, then My Position. The cameras go out on `rows` for the **Map** (wire the map's `sel` back to `select`: a click picks a camera). The kind comes from the address: a **picture** (`.jpg` / `.png`…) or an **MJPEG** stream (the first whole JPEG frame is cut out) is fetched, drawn in the node and sent on `img` (*picture width*), refreshed every *picture refresh* seconds (0 — once, *Refresh now*); an **HLS** stream goes out on `url` (wire it to **IPTV**); a **web page** is not a picture: *Open page* opens it. A picture URL can also be typed into *camera* or sent to the `url` input. Only cameras that someone entered into OpenStreetMap are found, and many of them are web pages.

**Free Music** (`freeMusic`, Music): *source* **Openverse** (Creative Commons tracks from Jamendo, Free Music Archive, Freesound, Wikimedia…; one file per result, 20 per page without a key; the licence and the source are in the row) or **Internet Archive** (a search over audio items, by default in the collections `opensource_audio` and `netlabels` — *extra search terms* is a Lucene fragment you can change). `rows` is what was found, *Next page ▶* the next page. In the Archive the results are albums: pick one in *album* (a name or the identifier) and its mp3 files (ogg / flac if there are none) become `tracks`; with Openverse `tracks` are the results. *track* (the number, or the `track` input with a number, a title part or an address), ◀ ▶ and the `next` pulse choose one: `url` and `name` (*artist — title*). Wire `tracks` to the Playlist's `rows` (the *stations* button adds them all). Without a key Openverse limits the number of requests, the node says so on HTTP 429.

Presets *Podcasts: Search → Episodes → Sound*, *Free Music: Openverse / Internet Archive → Playlist*, *Webcams: OpenStreetMap map → Picture*. Logic: `modules/online-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-online.mjs` (feeds, API answers, MJPEG, the nodes against a mocked network). **Not checked against the live services** (the sandbox it was written in has no outside network): the iTunes, Openverse, Internet Archive and Overpass request and answer formats follow their documentation.

**DVB USB (Astrometa)** (`dvbUsb`, Sources, WebUSB — Chrome / Edge): a DVB-T / T2 / C receiver on a USB stick with **RTL2832U + R828D + Panasonic MN88473 / MN88472 or Sony CXD2837ER** — the Astrometa DVB-T2 (`15f4:0131`; the kernel's `rtl28xxu` driver finds out which of the three demodulators is on the board, and so does this node) and sticks built the same way. The node brings the stick up the way the Linux drivers do, tunes it and puts the transport stream on `bin` for **TS → Video**; outputs `lock`, `cnr` (dB) and `level` (0…1) show the reception. Preset *Digital TV: DVB-T2 Astrometa → TS → Video and Sound*.

1. **Firmware** (Panasonic MN88473 / MN88472 only; the Sony CXD2837ER has none): the demodulator needs a file that is not bundled — `dvb-demod-mn88473-01.fw` (MN88473) or `dvb-demod-mn88472-02.fw` (MN88472), from the `linux-firmware` package (`/lib/firmware`). Choose it once in the node (parameter *fw*): it is kept in the browser's IndexedDB; it can also be placed in `vendor/` next to the app.
2. **Connect USB…:** the node finds the R828D tuner and the demodulator on the stick's I2C bus, loads the firmware and shows every step in its log. On Linux free the stick from the kernel first (`rmmod dvb_usb_rtl28xxu`), on Windows give it a WinUSB driver (Zadig).
3. **Tune:** *frequency* is the channel centre in MHz (UHF channel 21 = 474, step 8; VHF channels 5–12 are 7 MHz wide, 177.5 + 7 × (n − 5)), *bandwidth* 8 / 7 / 6 MHz, *standard* auto (DVB-T2, then DVB-T after 3 s without lock) or fixed, *PLP* for DVB-T2. A wire on the `freq` input retunes (a *Band Scanner* or a *Table* of channels). **Scan the air** walks over the channels *from…to* (default UHF 21–69; VHF III is 5–12; DVB-T2, then DVB-T on each), waits up to 1.6 s for a signal and up to 6 s for a lock on a channel that shows one, logs every channel with a signal and C/N, and leaves the node tuned to the first channel with a lock.

- **What is ported:** the sequences of the Linux drivers `rtl28xxu` (power, GPIO, endpoint, I2C commands), `rtl2832` (TS pass-through from the slave demodulator), `r820t` (tuner for DVB: filters, IF 3.57 / 4.57 MHz, PLL, input), `mn88473` and `mn88472` (firmware, tuning, lock, C/N), `cxd2841er` (Sony CXD2837ER: DVB-T, T2, C; 6 / 7 / 8 MHz; the register sequences are not typed in but **recorded by running the kernel's own functions** — `tools/cxd-gen/gen.py` cuts them out of `cxd2841er.c`, compiles them with a stub bus and writes `modules/cxd-sequences.js`; the state machine, the status and C/N are ported by hand; manual PLP selection of DVB-T2 and the signal level through the demodulator's AGC are not). Not ported: the tuner's image-rejection calibration (IMR — the kernel can run without it too), DVB-C on the MN88472 beyond the register set of the driver, signal-level measurement through the tuner.
- **Checked:** the tuner is compared with the kernel's own functions compiled in C (`tools/dvb-ref`): the sequence of I2C operations is identical for 9 tunings (DVB-T / T2 / C, 6 / 7 / 8 MHz, the special 506 / 666 / 818 MHz cases); the Sony sequences are the kernel's own output (a switch of the standard in the kernel is checked to be "sleep, then activate"; the tuner's IF equals the IF the sequences were recorded for); the demodulators, the RTL2832 and the firmware transfer run against an emulated bus (`node tools/test-dvb.mjs tools/dvb-ref/tuner_ref`), and the whole chain — connect, firmware, tune, lock, stream into *TS → Video* — against an emulated USB stick in a browser. **Not checked on a real stick** — nothing in the emulator can prove the registers do what they should on silicon. If it does not lock or the stream is empty, the node log shows the last step; send it along.
- Sources: GPL-2.0-or-later code of the Linux kernel (drivers/media/…, Antti Palosaari, Mauro Carvalho Chehab and others) — a port of the register sequences. `tools/dvb-ref/tuner_ref.c` is made of the kernel's `r820t.c` functions with a stub bus.

### DVB-S / DVB-S2 / DVB-T2 from IQ

**DVB Receiver (IQ)** (`dvbsoft`, Decoders): digital TV straight from the IQ of any SDR — no demodulator chip. One node with a *standard* choice (DVB-S, DVB-S2 and DVB-T2 now; every standard is an engine in the worker, DVB-T is the next one; for it today use **DVB USB (Astrometa)**). One node: `iq` in, picture and sound out. It demodulates, corrects errors and hands the transport stream to the same decoder as **TS → Video** (H.264 / HEVC / MPEG-2, MP2, teletext, service tables; the same parameters and the `prog` / `page` inputs). Outputs: `vid`, `L`, `R`, `bin` (the transport stream), `lock` (carrier and FEC locked), `level` (0…1, from −60 dBFS), `mer` (dB), `ber` (Viterbi bit error rate), `freq` (carrier offset, Hz), `vlock` (a picture is decoded) and the `ttx` / `ttxt` / `mux` / `chan` / `epg` / `info` outputs of TS → Video. Preset *Digital TV: DVB-S/S2 from IQ (USB SDR → one receiver node)*.

- **Settings:** *standard* (DVB-S: QPSK, rates 1/2…7/8; DVB-S2: QPSK / 8PSK / 16APSK / 32APSK, rates 1/4…9/10), *symbol rate* in kBd, *roll-off*, *offset* from the input centre (the carrier search covers about a quarter of the symbol rate), *force Viterbi* for weak signals. The input needs at least 2 samples per symbol; the sample rate is taken from the stream, a change restarts the demodulator. The signal level is not critical (AGC; locks over a 46 dB range in the test).
- **Practical use:** amateur DATV on QO-100 (333 kBd – 1.5 MBd; an RTL-SDR at 2.4 MS/s covers up to ~1.2 MBd), satellite feeds through an LNB (L-band IF). A broadcast 27.5 MBd carrier needs an SDR with 40+ MS/s (RX-888) and a fast machine; the demodulator runs in a worker and the node skips blocks (and says so) when it falls behind.
- **How it is built:** [LeanSDR](https://github.com/pabr/leansdr) `leandvb` (by *pabr*) compiled to WebAssembly (`vendor/leandvb.wasm`, GPL-3.0-or-later; `tools/leandvb/build.sh`, a small patch that lets the host step the scheduler instead of reading stdin to the end). The glue is `modules/leandvb-kernels.js`, the worker `dvbsoft-worker.js`, the node `modules/dvbsoft.js`.
- **DVB-T2** (`vendor/dvbofdm.wasm`, own receiver, `tools/dvbofdm`): any sample rate not below the channel rate (resampled inside); settings *bandwidth* (8 / 7 / 6 / 5 / 10 / 1.7 MHz) and *PLP* (-1 = first). Chain: P1 search, L1-pre / L1-post, channel estimate from the pilots, CFO / sampling drift tracking, de-interleaving, QAM demapper (also rotated constellations), LDPC (int8 layered min-sum) + BCH, BBFRAME → TS. FFT 1K…32K, all guard intervals, PP1…PP8 pilot patterns as the tables allow. The readout shows FFT / GI / PLP / modulation / SNR / FEC blocks. Build: `tools/dvbofdm/build.sh <gnuradio checkout>` (tables are cut from gr-dtv); test: `node tools/test-dvbt2.mjs <cfile> <ref.ts> [bw] [rate]` (bit-exact TS compare against a GNU Radio transmitter).
- **DVB-T2 checked:** GNU Radio gr-dtv profiles (16K / 32K, GI 1/32…19/128, PP2 / PP3 / PP4, QPSK / 64QAM / 256QAM, rates 1/2…2/3, rotated 64 / 256QAM) bit-exact; noise down to SNR ≈ 11–12 dB (64QAM 1/2), CFO ±15 kHz, ±50 ppm, echoes up to 0.8 at 1000 samples. **Not checked** (no reference signal): type-2 PLP, common PLP, time interleaving over several frames, MISO, PAPR reduction, FEF, T2-Lite, a real transmitter. One thread decodes 3–5 MS/s on a clean signal (32K 64QAM), so a full 8 MHz channel (9.14 MS/s) goes through a **pool of workers**: the receiver worker only searches the preamble, decodes L1-pre and puts the stream together in the order of the frames, while whole frames (demodulation, L1-post, FEC) run in `dvbsoft-pool.js` workers, one frame per worker. The parameter *workers* is -1 for automatic (cores − 2, at most 6; fewer than 2 means one thread) or a number; if all workers are busy the frame is skipped and the readout says so. Measured on 4 cores: 1 / 2 / 3 workers decode 4.5 / 6.5 / 8.9 MS/s of a 32K 64QAM signal, bit-exact; a time interleaver over several frames needs the previous frames, so such a PLP is decoded in the receiver worker alone. Test with real threads: `node tools/test-dvbt2-par.mjs <cfile> <ref.ts> <workers> [bw] [rate]`; `PAR=n LAG=k node tools/test-dvbt2.mjs …` runs the same job protocol with delayed, reordered results.
- **Tests:** `node tools/test-leandvb.mjs <leansdr checkout>` builds the LeanSDR transmitter and feeds its signal to the wasm module: DVB-S QPSK 1/2, 2/3, 3/4, 7/8, 2 and 3 samples per symbol, with noise (MER 11.7 dB), and a real MPEG-2 + MP2 stream from ffmpeg through the transmitter into the demultiplexer (all frames, no lost packets). The node itself was run in Chromium on the same signal (lock, MER, transport stream on `bin`). **Not tested:** DVB-S2 (it is compiled in, but LeanSDR's transmitter makes only DVB-S framing, so there is no test signal here), a real satellite, an SDR in the loop, 8PSK / APSK, Fs/Fm below 2.
