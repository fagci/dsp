# SDR and receivers

USB SDRs directly in the browser, remote receivers (rtl_tcp, SpyServer, KiwiSDR, OpenWebRX), tinySA, telemetry, Remote ID, ESP-SDR.

## USB SDR

The **USB SDR** node talks to the receiver directly over WebUSB (Chrome, Edge, Opera), no drivers or native software needed. Up to 4 demodulator channels share one device.
- **Reliable start of RTL-SDR (Blog V4 and others).** The tuner PLL sometimes does not lock after a cold start. A pause after the tuner initialisation; a retune that does not hand the receiver over without a PLL lock (repeats with growing pauses, the last one after a full re-initialisation of the tuner with the gain restored); the IF filter calibration is repeated when it does not converge, as in librtlsdr.
- **Tuner operations run one at a time.** Retune, gain, lock check and the like used to interleave inside the USB worker, and one of them could close the I2C gate while another was writing: the write was lost on the way to the chip, and the Blog V4 input stayed from the previous frequency (a deaf, «wrong» spectrum after a page reload that only Disconnect / Connect cured). They now go strictly through a queue. One dongle belongs to one node: connecting a second USB SDR node to it cleanly takes the dongle over.
- **No hard clipping of the sound.** FM is scaled so that the nominal deviation gives 1.0, while noise between transmissions, over-deviation and AM / SSB without AGC give 2–3 times more — the output was cut hard (a rasp). A soft limiter on the output of every mode: linear up to 0.7, above it smoothly towards 1.0. `OVERLOAD` in the readout is a different thing — the ADC itself is overdriven: lower the gain.

Modes: WFM (stereo and RDS on request, see below), NFM, AM, SAM, USB, LSB, raw IQ. **SAM** (synchronous AM) locks a PLL onto the carrier and detects coherently: less distortion during selective fading, and **SAM sideband** = USB/LSB keeps only one sideband to dodge interference on the other. In AM/SAM/SSB the tuner is always parked sr/4 away from the channel, so the DC spike never lands on the carrier.

For HF: **noise blanker** cuts short impulses (power-line, switching supplies) on the raw wideband IQ before the channel filter, where a pulse is still short; **auto notch** (NLMS predictor) removes steady whistles/heterodynes from AM/SAM/SSB audio; **SAM sideband = ISB** puts the upper sideband in the left channel and the lower in the right, so you hear which side the interference is on.

Common controls: gain (auto or manual), bias-tee, ppm correction, center shift off DC. Manual gain works only with *auto* off — moving the gain slider (or wiring its input) switches *auto* off by itself. On R820T / R828D (incl. RTL-SDR Blog V4) the LNA and mixer steps follow librtlsdr's table exactly (29 steps, 0–49.6 dB, VGA 16.3 dB); the slider rounds up to the next step.

**ADC overload**: the status line shows the ADC peak and mean power in dBFS over the last ~0.5 s, and **⚠ OVERLOAD** (held for 3 s) when more than 0.01% of I/Q samples sit on the ADC rails. With an 8-bit RTL-SDR a strong local station easily does that, and the spectrum then fills with intermod products that are not real signals — lower the gain until the warning goes away; a mean level around −30…−15 dBFS is usually a good spot. Outputs `adcPk`, `adcRms` (dBFS), `clip` (% of samples) and `ovl` (0/1) let a patch react, e.g. gate a detector or step the gain down.

**WFM: stereo and RDS.** Both are off the CPU until you ask for them: *WFM stereo* is **off** by default (mono is enough for most listening and saves the 19 kHz pilot PLL and the L−R filter), and the RDS decoder runs **only while something is wired to the `rds` output** (or to `ps` / `rt`, which still work as before). `rds` is a `rec` output: one record per change of what has been received (at most four a second) — `{t, src:'RDS', kind:'rds', id, freq, pi, call, ps, rt, pty, ptyName, tp, ta, ms, country, bler, snap}`, where `snap` is the full decoded state. Wire it into **RDS Display**, or into a Table / Rec Log for a log of stations. Retuning clears the state.

**RDS Display** (Radio) draws what has been decoded: the station name (PS), PI with the US call sign (RBDS: K/W stations) and the coverage area, the country (from ECC and PI; only ECC E0…E3 are in the table), the share of good blocks, the flags **TP, TA, music / speech and DI** (stereo, artificial head, compressed, dynamic PTY; dashed = not received yet), RadioText, **RT+** (artist, title and other tags, `♪` line), programme type (RDS or RBDS names) and PTYN, the station clock with its UTC offset, the programme item number (PIN), ECC / language code, **alternative frequencies** (FM, LF / MF; the tuned one is lit), applications announced in group 3A (RT+, TMC, …), other networks (**EON**: PS, PTY, TA, AF) and a bar chart of the groups received (0A … 15B). Records of several stations (a scanner) become chips at the bottom: click one to pin it. Outputs: `ps`, `rt`, `artist`, `title`, `pty`, `sync`. The layout follows the size of the node or the dashboard tile: from one line with the name and the radiotext in a narrow strip to five columns of cards with all lists on a wide tile.

The *IQ Demodulator* block has its own, simpler RDS (name and radiotext only).

The `spec` output is updated at the *fps* rate (16 ms at *fps max*, 50 ms at *auto*, 100 ms at *min*): every update is one FFT over the latest N samples, and **spectrum averaging** is an exponential smoothing of the power over that many frames (`all` = 64, `off` = no smoothing), as in SDR++. The noise floor spread drops by ~√frames and weak carriers stand out; detectors (CFAR, channel SNR) can run with a lower threshold. Levels are in dBFS: 0 dB is a full-scale complex tone, whatever the window. **spectrum FFT size** goes from 512 to 262144 (256 k) bins: at 2.4 MS/s that is 9 Hz per bin, enough to resolve narrow carriers and their drift; a big size updates less often (one 262144-point frame takes tens of milliseconds, so expect a few frames per second). The frequency axis is kept in 64-bit floats, so deep zoom on GHz signals stays smooth

**Signal level and squelch** (per channel): RSSI is the power inside the channel filter in dBFS (for FM and SSB a separate sharp band filter is used for the measurement, so neighbours in the transition band don't count); SNR is taken from the spectrum — mean level inside the channel against the quieter of the two bands just outside it, so a busy neighbour on one side does not raise the noise estimate. **squelch** mutes a channel by `SNR` (does not depend on gain) or by `level` (RSSI threshold, dBFS), with 3 dB hysteresis and a **hang** time; the gate is applied to the audio exactly at the chunk the level was measured on. Outputs `rssi`/`snr` (and `rssi2…4`, `snr2…4` for the other channels) and `sqOpen` for channel 1, e.g. to start a recorder. On a Spectrum Analyzer wired to `spec` the channel band shows the squelch in the trace's own scale: a dashed line at the threshold and a solid line at the current mean level in the channel (solid above dashed — open, the band label says `SQL open/closed`); a marker sitting on a channel shows that channel's RSSI (dBFS) / SNR — exactly the numbers the squelch compares — instead of the peak level around the marker.

Fine marker tuning (e.g. SSB): the mouse wheel over a marker's label moves it by 10 Hz (Ctrl — 1 Hz, Shift — 100 Hz); dragging the label moves it at 1/10 of the normal pan speed; clicking the active marker's label asks for an exact frequency.

- **HackRF** has separate LNA / VGA / amp controls instead of the gain slider.
- **HackRF TX** (IQ category): transmits any `iq` stream. The air frequency is the stream's centre frequency plus the signal offset, e.g. in IQ Generator / AM/FM/SSB Modulator `fc` + `off`; the sample rate also comes from the stream (2–20 MS/s). Keep `off` away from 0 (e.g. 100 kHz): HackRF has a carrier spike at the centre. Audio → radio: *any audio node → **AM/FM/SSB Modulator** (AM / NFM / WFM / USB / LSB, audio ±1 = full deviation or depth) → HackRF TX*; for baseband I/Q already in the engine use *I/Q → IQ*. The node has TX VGA (0–47 dB) and amp +14 dB, both off by default. It takes the whole device, so do not keep the same HackRF open in USB SDR. If the stream is slower than the USB, zeros (no carrier) go out and *underruns* counts them. **You must comply with local radio regulations: transmit only where and how the law allows.** Transmission is also switched by the **`tx` pin** (a number ≥ 0.5 = on): it starts on a rising edge (a pin that is already high when the patch loads does nothing), stops on a falling edge or when the wire is removed. Safety: with no data for 0.4 s (graph stopped, source silent) the driver turns the RF path off completely — a stream of zeros would leave the LO carrier — and transmits again when data returns; removing the node, Disconnect, Stop and closing the tab all send the stop command (on a crash or USB unplug the firmware cannot be told, so check the transmitter after one). Test into a dummy load with the amp off.
- **SDRplay / MSi2500** has no hardware AGC: *auto* sets a fixed 62 dB, the manual slider covers 0–102 dB of LNA + mixer + baseband gain. The driver is a port of [libmirisdr-4](https://github.com/f4exb/libmirisdr-4). RSP1A (`1df7:3020`) / RSP2 IDs are recognized but untested; RSPduo, RSPdx and newer models need the closed SDRplay API and are not supported. On Android Chrome an RSP1 may expose only alternate setting 0 (no streaming endpoint), so it works on desktop only; try a direct OTG cable without a hub.
- **RX-888** (and other SDDC boards: HF103, BBRF103): on power-up it is an empty Cypress FX3 boot loader; Connect uploads the firmware (`vendor/SDDC_FX3.img` from [ExtIO_sddc](https://github.com/ik1xpv/ExtIO_sddc), MIT) and the board comes back as a new USB device — if the browser does not pick it up by itself, press Connect again and choose it. On HF the ADC samples the antenna directly: the ADC clock is the sample rate × 2^k (up to 66 MHz over USB 3, 16 MHz over USB 2), and HF is everything below half of it — 1.024, 2.048 or 8 MSPS give the whole 0–30 MHz, 2.4 MSPS only up to 19 MHz, and tuning and decimation to the selected sample rate run in the browser (NCO + half-band filters, 80 dB alias rejection) spread over several workers. Above the ADC's Nyquist frequency the VHF tuner is used (R820T / R828D; mkIII — RDA5815 from 220 MHz). Gain on HF: the slider first removes the 0–31.5 dB attenuator, then raises the AD8370 VGA; *auto* = no attenuation, +10 dB VGA. On Windows the board needs the WinUSB driver (Zadig) for both the boot loader and the running device. Untested on real hardware yet.

On Linux unload the kernel driver before connecting, e.g. `sudo rmmod msi001 msi2500`, or blacklist it in `/etc/modprobe.d/`. The device also needs user access through a udev rule (as for `rtl-sdr` / `hackrf` / `airspy` packages).

### Wideband sweep

**wideband sweep** turns the node into a panoramic scanner (like `rtl_power` / `hackrf_sweep`): the receiver steps across **sweep from … to** (MHz), each step keeps the central **usable band fraction** of the FFT (the edges are rolled off by the anti-alias filter), and the pieces are stitched into one spectrum on the `spec` output. A Spectrum Analyzer on it shows the whole range; its waterfall gets one line per full pass. The readout shows the current step and seconds per line.

- **sweep step overlap** (default 20 %): neighbouring steps overlap by that share of the usable band and are cross-faded there (linear in dB), so the edge roll-off and small gain differences between steps do not leave a staircase. Costs proportionally more steps per pass; 0 gives the old edge-to-edge stitching
- **smoothing between lines** (advanced) averages every step with its value from the previous pass (exponential, 0 = off) — a calmer noise floor on slow sweeps
- the `specPeak` output is a second spectrum on the same axis: the maximum of every bin over all passes (it restarts when the range or FFT size changes). **specPeak decay** forgets it slowly (dB per line, 0 = hold) — short transmissions that hit only one pass stay visible and can feed a detector or a log; the Spectrum Analyzer's own *peak hold* does the same on screen only
- **sweep FFT size** sets the resolution (sample rate / size per bin), **averages per step** — how many FFT frames are averaged (or max-held with the **max** detector) at each step
- a marker on `tuneFreq` (e.g. marker 1 of the Spectrum Analyzer wired to it) pauses the sweep and tunes there: the demodulators play as usual and the live spectrum is drawn over its part of the panorama; remove the marker to resume from the same step
- with AGC on, the sweep switches the tuner to the fixed **gain, dB** for the pass (each step with its own AGC level would stripe the waterfall) and returns the AGC afterwards; HackRF has no AGC. **shift center off DC** keeps the listened station away from the DC spike
- **hardware sweep** (HackRF only, advanced): the firmware retunes by itself (`hackrf_sweep` mode, 20 MSPS, 5 MHz steps from a whole MHz, 6 GHz max) and every USB block carries its frequency, so a pass takes a fraction of a second instead of tens of milliseconds per step. There is one FFT per block (size up to 4096); **averages per step** then means passes per waterfall line, **usable band fraction** and **settle time** do not apply. A marker on `tuneFreq` still pauses the sweep and returns the receiver to normal mode. If no blocks arrive for 2 s the option switches itself off and the software sweep runs. Not checked on real hardware yet — the block format is taken from libhackrf
- the speed depends on the retune time over USB: roughly 20–50 ms per step, i.e. a few seconds per line for 100 MHz at 2.4 MSPS and ~30–45 s for the whole RTL-SDR range. A HackRF at 20 MSPS covers ~8× more per step
- the Spectrum Analyzer keeps the waterfall history at full resolution (**waterfall history memory**, 128 MB by default), so zooming into an old part of the panorama shows the real bins, not stretched pixels. Over the budget the oldest lines keep only a max-decimated copy The frequency axis of *IQ Spectrum*, *IQ Channelizer* and *Zoom-FFT* is kept in 64-bit floats, and the analyzer finds the bin of a column from the ends of a regular axis: a 32-bit axis resolves only 128 Hz at 1.5 GHz (8 Hz at 100 MHz), coarser than an FFT bin, which showed as steps and blocks in the live rows after a deep zoom while the redrawn history stayed smooth
- ready-made patch: **USB SDR: Wideband Sweep**
- **Channel Scanner** (Radio): scans a list of channels with the fewest receiver retunes. `bands` takes a Table (rows with `freq`, or `lo` / `hi` + `step`, which are expanded into channels), `skip` is a blacklist (rows with `freq` or `lo` / `hi`; a point matches within 1 kHz). The window width comes from the receiver (`freqLo` / `freqHi`) minus the dead zone *edge, %* on each side; the channels are grouped greedily left to right, which gives the minimum number of windows. The receiver is retuned (`freq`) only between windows; the channels of a window are visited by the digital tuner (`tune` → `tuneFreq`). The squelch (`active`, e.g. the receiver's `sqOpen` with squelch on) stops the scan on a channel: it stays *hang* ms after the squelch closes (reopening restarts the wait), at most *timeout* ms. *settle* is the wait after a window retune, *dwell* the time on a channel before the squelch is read. With `spec` wired (the receiver's spectrum) the scanner first collects *frames* spectrum frames of the new window (the first one after a retune is dropped), takes the level of every channel (peak within *channel width*) over the window noise (median) and checks the squelch only on channels above *spectrum check, dB* — quiet channels cost no *dwell*; 0 turns it off, channels within 3 kHz of the window centre (DC spike) are always checked by the squelch, and with no fresh frames all channels are checked. `plan` gives the windows as bands for the analyzer, `label` the channel name. Preset: **USB SDR: Channel Scan (Channel Scanner + squelch)**
- **Decoder Switch** (Decoders): sends a channel's IQ (`in`, after IQ Frequency Shift + IQ Decimator) to one of up to 4 branches `out1…out4` — a decoder for each kind of signal. While there is a signal (`active`: the Channel Scanner's `listening` or an IQ Squelch's `open`; unwired = always) the branches are tried in turn, *time on a branch* ms each; a branch whose decoder shows life on its `ev1…ev4` input (records, or a number > 0; for the Digital Voice Decoder wire its `lock` — it is 1 while any protocol is in sync, voice or data, so a DMR channel with only data / CSBK / SMS stays on the digital branch and is not played as analog noise; `voice` would lock only on voice frames) is locked and kept until it stays quiet for *keep* ms. If nothing is recognised in one round, the *fallback* branch (2 by default — analog FM) takes the rest of the transmission; no signal — all branches are empty and the search starts over with the next one. Manual: the `sel` input (1…4) or the *fixed branch* parameter. Outputs `sel` (branch number), `name` and `locked`. The first chunk after a branch change is tagged `gap`, so a decoder restarts cleanly. Preset **USB SDR: Channel Scan + Decoder Switch (digital voice / analog)**: Channel Scanner → IQ Squelch → switch → branch 1 Digital Voice Decoder (DMR, P25, NXDN, YSF, D-STAR, dPMR, M17 by sync words) → Vocoder (mbelib) → sound, branch 2 FM audio.
- **VDL Mode 2 Decoder** (Decoders, `modules/vdl2.js` + `vdl2-kernels.js`): the air-ground data link of civil aviation, 136.650–136.975 MHz, D8PSK 10500 Bd. IQ of the channel (≥ 48 kS/s, the carrier within ±1–2 kHz of the centre) → low-pass filter 8 kHz → 105 kS/s → search for the 16-symbol preamble (the linear regression of the phase error also measures the frequency error, `df`) → differential D8PSK with Gray code → scrambler x^15+x+1 → 25-bit header (burst length, single-error correction by the syndrome table) → RS(255,249) over interleaved blocks (the short last block has 0 / 2 / 4 / 6 check bytes, the rest are erasures; up to 3 wrong bytes per block are corrected) → HDLC with bit stuffing → AVLC frames with the FCS (addresses ICAO 24 bit, types I / S / U). ACARS blocks in I-frames (`ff ff 01 …`) come out as records `kind:'message'` with the fields of **ACARS Decoder** (`reg`, `label`, `blk`, `flight`, `no`, `txt`) plus `from` / `to` / `rssi` / `df` / `fixed`; **XID** frames come out as `kind:'xid'` with the type by the C/R, P/F and connection-management bits (`GSIF` — the ground station beacon with its location, nearest airport and the frequency list with the stations' addresses; `XID_CMD_LE` / `XID_RSP_LE` link establishment with the destination airport, the aircraft position and altitude; handoff, LPM, link connection refused with the cause) and the parameters as `xid.p`; the other I-frames are **X.25** (mod 8, `kind:'x25'`: call request / accepted with the addresses and the SNDCF, data with the sequence numbers and the reassembly of `more` packets, clear / reset / restart with the cause, diagnostics) with the **CLNP** inside (full header with the NSAPs and the offset-based reassembly of the segments, or the X.233 compressed header with `lref`), then **COTP** (CR / CC / DR / DT with `eot` and `seq`, AK, …) or **ES-IS** (ES / IS Hello with the NET) and the user data as hex; **not decoded**: the ASN.1 applications above COTP (CM, CPDLC, ADS-C), IDRP and the XID/X.25 facility fields (hex only). Algorithms and constants follow dumpvdl2 (GPLv3) and libacars; it is tested against a transmitter built from the same description (`tools/test-vdl2.mjs`: noise, ±300 Hz offset, 48–250 kS/s, FEC with erasures), not against a real recording. The IQ Generator has the **VDL2** mode; patch **VDL Mode 2: ACARS over D8PSK (Generator)**.
- **Universal Decoder** (Decoders): one node instead of a row of decoders with a switch — the IQ of a channel goes in, one stream of records with a `proto` field comes out (`rec`, `text`, `proto`, `locked`). Inside is a registry of decoders on the IQK kernels (`UDEC_REG` in `udec-kernels.js`: AIS, POCSAG, ISM 433 (a narrow channel — **ISM 433 Decoder**, it hears only ± half its width around the centre; a stream wider than 600 kS/s, e.g. the whole LPD 433.05–434.79 MHz in the SDR window — **ISM Scanner (rtl_433)**, channel by channel; the hint `ism` picks by the width of the stream too), ACARS, digital voice (DMR / P25 / NXDN / YSF / M17 / D-STAR / dPMR — **Digital Voice Decoder**), MPT 1327, TETRA, RS41; each has its bands: with the frequency of the stream known (`fc`, e.g. after **IQ Frequency Shift**) only the decoders of that band listen, with an unknown one — all); each gets its own state, created lazily and dropped when not needed. Modes: *manual* — the protocol you pick; *hint* — the `hint` input names it (a decoder id, a protocol or a catalog kind, e.g. the `decoder` output of **Signal Type Identifier** / **Signal Database**); *auto* — with no hint all protocols listen at once, the first to decode stays and the node goes back to the search after *silence* ms. A hinted decoder that does not decode within *giveUp* ms is taken for a wrong hint and ignored until the hint changes (the Signal ID guesses by the width and the range, it can be wrong). A new protocol is one entry of `UDEC_REG` (its default parameters are copied from the module: the worker does not see the module descriptions; `tools/test-udec.mjs` checks they match) and one of the signal catalog. Adapters: a protocol can be a chain of two parts — **ADS-B** is the demodulator of the frames plus the table of aircraft (`adsb-kernels.js`, shared with **ADS-B Decoder**): the node outputs ready aircraft records for the map (position, altitude, speed, callsign; `rlat` / `rlon` — receiver position) and needs a stream of ≥ 2 MS/s, so feed it the wide IQ of the SDR without decimation (decoders that lack the stream width do not listen). **LoRa** has no single setting, so typical networks are separate entries: **LoRaWAN** (SF7, 125 kHz, sync 0x34) and **Meshtastic** (SF11, 250 kHz, sync 0x2B), the frame is decoded by the one that matches; `proto` says which (hints: `lorawan`, `lora` / `meshAir`). Patch **Universal Decoder: ADS-B Aircraft and LoRa Frames in One Node (Generator)**. **VDL Mode 2** is an entry of the registry too (`vdl2Rx`, band 136–137 MHz, hints `vdl2` / `vdlm2` / `vdl`). So is **Inmarsat Aero** (`aeroRx`, band 1525–1559 MHz, hints `aero` / `inmarsat` / `jaero`, P600 by default). **Main-thread decoders** (entries with `main:true` in `UDEC_REG`, bridge `modules/udec-audio.js`): **SAME / EAS** (NFM, 162.4–162.55 MHz), **SELCAL** (USB, HF air band), **Morse** (envelope of the carrier, no demodulator: keying of a carrier at any offset), **DTMF** (NFM), **RTTY** (Baudot) and **aFSK (ASCII)** (USB; **aFSK: Auto-Receive** finds tones and speed itself, text goes out only with a locked speed and printable characters, otherwise as `unknown` records), **AX.25 / APRS** (NFM → FSK Demodulator → Receive AX.25/HDLC, records with `from`, `to`, `path`, `info`), **WWV / WWVH / CHU** (AM, time code), **FT8**, **JS8Call**, **Olivia 8/250**, **Olivia 16/500** (USB; slots of 15 s by the UTC clock, the dial is the stream frequency `fc`), **TBSK** (acoustic modem; never in the search), **GSM** (IQ straight into **GSM: Receive Bursts**, a stream ≥ 1.06 MS/s) — they are blocks of the sound domain (`Eng.sr`, `BLOCK`) with no kernel in the worker. The bridge cuts the channel with **IQ Decimator**, demodulates it (FM / AM / USB / envelope, one chain for all entries of the same kind), cuts blocks of sound and runs the block with `Eng.sr` and `BLOCK` swapped for the length of the call. Such a node is computed in the main thread (`mainWhen` of the descriptor): it moves there by itself when the hint or the manual choice names one of these decoders, or by the *audio* check; the IQ decoders of the same node stay as fast as before only while it is off. In the auto search they listen only when the stream frequency (`fc`) is known and falls into their band (the range lists of `UDEC_REG`): a demodulator for each of them without a frequency is too much, and noise decoders (Morse, aFSK) give false text. **Meteor LRPT** is an IQ adapter and works in the worker: PSK Demodulator → CCSDS Decoder → the frames of VC5 are collected into the MSU-MR picture (`video` output, an RGB composite every 2 s), the records are the frames without the raw bytes. Not in the node: **HFDL** (a graph of ten blocks with a feedback loop, see the notes of its patch), **Chirp Modem** (a lab modem: symbols without framing) and **Protocol Decoder (4FSK)** (already inside **Digital Voice**). ### IQ recording and playback

- **● Record IQ** writes the stream to a temporary file in the browser's site storage (OPFS, so long recordings don't sit in memory; without it — up to 1 GB in memory); **■ Stop recording** opens the save dialog (or downloads the file). If saving was cancelled or the recording stopped on its own (error, disconnect), press Stop again to save it
  - **WAV** — 2-channel 16-bit PCM with the plain 44-byte header SDR++ expects (8-bit sources are widened to 16 bit); center frequency in the file name (`baseband_<Hz>Hz_…`, SDR++) and in an `auxi` chunk after the data (SDR#, HDSDR); limited to 4 GB
  - **SigMF** — `.sigmf` archive (`.sigmf-data` + `.sigmf-meta`), retuning while recording adds a new `captures` segment
  - **Part of the band:** *IQ record band* = `channel 1` (around its tuning) or `custom` (*IQ record centre*) records only *IQ record bandwidth* — the stream is shifted, low-pass filtered and decimated, so the file is small and holds the chosen centre and rate (16-bit; the centre is in the name and in the metadata)
  - **Tags and annotations:** *tag* goes into the file name, SigMF `core:description` and the labels of marks. Marks (a short annotation around channel 1 at the current place) are set by the node inputs, like *text* + *go* of Text Source: **`markTrg`** (a rising edge, e.g. `go` of Text Source, a Timer or a Constant) sets a mark and **`mark`** is its text (e.g. a frame type from a decoder; empty — the *tag*). The label is the text plus the local time (*marks: add local time*). The **`rec`** input takes records of a decoder (`rec` output): every record sets a mark named automatically (`label`, else `source kind id`), or with your own text if one is wired to `mark`. Marks are drawn as boxes with a label on the Spectrum Analyzer waterfall and written to SigMF as `annotations` (time, frequency edges, label). WAV has no place for annotations
  - **Record on trigger:** *record while squelch is open* — the recording starts when the channel 1 squelch opens and stops when it closes (*squelch hang* applies) or at *max length*; needs squelch on. *Pre-roll* keeps the last seconds before the trigger. A triggered recording is saved by download, with no dialog. **Record trigger: go input** does the same from the **`recGo`** input (a number above 0.5 records) — e.g. the `go` of [Satellite Autopilot](#satellite-autopilot) for a satellite pass; pre-roll and max length apply, squelch is not needed. The node has to be processed (wired or the engine running), as the trigger works on its ticks
- **Open IQ file…** plays a recording through the same chain (spectrum, 4 channels, demodulators) in real time, with loop. A bar under the frequency (playback runs with the global Run button) shows progress and the file's annotations: click or drag to seek, **❚❚ / ▶** pauses, **−10 / +10** jump 10 s, **|◂ / ▸|** go to the previous / next annotation. SigMF annotations are loaded and shown on the waterfall
  - WAV (8/16-bit PCM, 32/64-bit float), SigMF archive or `.sigmf-meta` + `.sigmf-data` pair (`cu8`, `ci8`, `ci16_le`, `cf32_le`, `cf64_le`)
  - raw `.cu8` / `.cs8` / `.cs16` / `.cf32` / `.cf64` (e.g. `rtl_sdr` output; the format is guessed from the extension or set by **IQ file format**): frequency and rate are taken from the file name (`…_433920000Hz_2.4Msps.cf32`), otherwise from the node settings
- **IQ in Files:** *IQ record goes to* = `Files` (default) saves the recording to the app storage — **Files › IQ** (*IQ record: folder in Files*) — instead of the save dialog / download; `file / download` is the old behaviour. A triggered recording (squelch, `recGo`) is saved the same way. **Open IQ from Files…** plays a stored recording through the same chain as **Open IQ file…**. The Files manager shows an IQ recording with the **length** (right), then the **centre frequency · sample rate · size on disk · #tags** under the name; the preview has the details (segments, source) and two fields — **tags** (comma separated) and a **note** — saved on change; search finds tags too. The metadata is kept in the Files record (`meta.iq`, `meta.tags`, `meta.note`); *tag* of the recorder takes several tags (the first goes into the file name, all go to SigMF `core:description`). Exporting a WAV from Files (**⤓**) gives the plain file; the tags live in the app (in a SigMF the ones given at recording time are in the file)
- **IQ Recorder** (Output, `modules/iqfiles.js`, logic `modules/iqfiles-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-iqfiles.mjs`) records any `iq` stream of the graph into Files: **whole input** (the stream as it comes — a whole band of an SDR, any block output) or a **channel** (shifted by *offset* or the `freq` input — an absolute frequency — and decimated to *bandwidth*; the file holds the channel centre and its own rate, 16 bit). *What goes in*: **everything**, **go input** (while `go` is above 0.5) or **voice only** — the parts of the stream marked by the `voice` records of a Digital Voice Decoder (DMR bursts: every record carries the place `st` in the input stream, so the cut is exact), joined into **one file**; *margin before / after* extends each part (a decoder needs the sync word and the header before the voice to lock again on playback — with 300 ms before, about three quarters of the voice bursts decode again from the cut file, with 40 ms about half; the test measures it). The recorder must sit on the same wire as the decoder (records from another wire fall back to the arrival time, less exact). WAV or SigMF; in SigMF every voice part is a `captures` entry (with its time) and an annotation (`from → to`, slot, the channel edges). The file name has the first tag, centre frequency and UTC time; the tags also get `voice`, `channel` and the protocol (`DMR`). *record as soon as the stream arrives* starts without a click, *start a new file after, MB* splits long sessions (the file is kept in memory until saved), **■ Stop and save** saves what is collected (a node removed from the graph saves too). ## Remote SDR

The **IQ over Network** node gives the same `iq` output as the USB SDR, so every IQ block (shift, decimator, demodulators, spectrum, decoders) works on a receiver that sits somewhere else: a Raspberry Pi on the roof, a server, another PC. A browser cannot open TCP sockets, so the receiver's stream goes through a small bridge to WebSocket; from the https page only `wss://` works, from a local copy over http `ws://` too.

- **rtl_tcp**: on the machine with the RTL-SDR run `rtl_tcp -a 0.0.0.0 -s 1024000` and a bridge next to it, e.g. `websockify 8766 127.0.0.1:1234` (any proxy that passes bytes will do), enter `ws://host:8766`. The node checks the `RTL0` header, shows the tuner (R820T…), sends the sample rate, frequency, gain (0 — auto), ppm, RTL AGC, bias-T and the direct sampling branch, and sends again whatever changes — also when a wire drives the frequency (the stream is tagged `retune`).
- **rtl_tcp (DSP app)**: the standard `rtl_tcp`, no `websockify` — the DSP app bridge on the phone (`ws://127.0.0.1:8765` from the page inside the app, or `@name` from Settings → Devices from another device), or `node tools/node/bridge.mjs` on a computer, opens the TCP connection itself (`tcp.connect`, as *Network (DSP app)* does) and passes the stream to the page in binary frames `[2][connection][bytes]` (the old app: records with hex, slower but works). Set *host* and *port* of the server. **On the phone**: an rtl_tcp driver app (the RTL-SDR on the phone over OTG) listens on `127.0.0.1:1234` — leave host `127.0.0.1`. **A Raspberry Pi / another computer**: run `rtl_tcp -a 0.0.0.0 -s 1024000` there and put its address in *host*. The commands (rate, frequency, gain, ppm, AGC, bias-T, direct sampling) go the same way. A page on another device than the phone needs *Settings → Let any page use actions* in the app; the node says so if the app refuses. Preset: *Remote SDR: FM Receiver (rtl_tcp via DSP app — phone driver, Raspberry Pi)*.
- **rtl_tcp (WebRTC)**: no bridge and no server port. The device with the SDR runs the **rtl_tcp Server** node (below), the receiving page picks this protocol, wires *WebRTC Data → link* to the `rtc` input of both nodes and connects them (offer / answer, or the auto-connect room). The same `RTL0` header and 5-byte commands travel over the `rtl` data channel of the connection, encrypted by WebRTC (DTLS). Press *Start* on the server, *Connect* on the client.
- **SpyServer** (Airspy One / HF+ / RTL-SDR behind Airspy's `spyserver`): same bridge, e.g. `websockify 8766 127.0.0.1:5555`. The sample rate is not free here: the server decimates by powers of two from its maximum, the node picks the nearest step to *sample rate* and uses the real one downstream (the readout shows it). Frequency and *SpyServer gain index* (−1 leaves the server's own) are sent on change; the server's digital gain, reported in each message, is divided out so levels stay true. IQ is requested as int16. A server that gives view-only access (no control) shows it in the status.
- **raw stream**: bytes of interleaved I / Q with the format, sample rate and center frequency set by hand. Examples: `rtl_sdr -f 100e6 -s 1024000 - | websocat -b -s 8766`, `hackrf_transfer -r - -f 100000000 -s 2000000 | websocat -b -s 8766` (set *int8*), GNU Radio or SoapySDR with a pipe sink, your own script. Samples that arrive cut in the middle (a byte boundary in a WebSocket message) are put together.
- The queue holds 2 seconds; when the page cannot keep up the oldest data is dropped, the stream is tagged `gap` and the overflow counter in the readout grows. The readout shows the real received rate next to the set one.

### Sharing your SDR as rtl_tcp (rtl_tcp Server)

The **rtl_tcp Server (any SDR)** node (IQ) makes a USB SDR look like `rtl_tcp` to other programs. Wire *USB SDR → iq* to its `iq` input; its outputs `freq`, `sr`, `gain` (dB), `ppm`, `agc`, `bias`, `direct` go back to the SDR (`freq`, `sr`, `gainDb` inputs), so the client tunes your radio. Any SDR with an `iq` output works (HackRF, Airspy…): the client is told the tuner type and gain steps you set in the node.
- **WebRTC**: the client is *IQ over Network* with *rtl_tcp (WebRTC)* on another device (see above).
- **TCP port (phone app / desktop bridge)**: the node asks a bridge with the DSP app's commands to open a real TCP port (`tcp.listen`, the same command as in *Network (DSP app)*), default 1234. On Android the DSP app itself is that bridge — set *bridge* to `ws://127.0.0.1:8765` (or `@name` from Settings → Devices) and nothing else has to run; on a computer run `node tools/node/bridge.mjs` (`--host 0.0.0.0 --token secret` for the LAN; the address is then `ws://host:8765/?token=secret`, or `@name` from Settings → Devices, where the address and the token are stored; the same picker is in *IQ over Network*, *Packet Link*, *Text over Network*, *Sensor (WebSocket)*, *MQTT*, *Rig Control* and *Rotator*). In SDR++ / GQRX pick the source *RTL-TCP*, the address of that device and the port. One client at a time; press *Stop* (or close the patch) to release the port; *allow other devices on the network* off keeps it to that device only. The newest app takes the IQ as **binary WebSocket frames** `[1][connection u32 BE][bytes]` (no hex, no reply per block: the same Wi-Fi traffic as the real `rtl_tcp` ×2 — to the phone and back to the laptop; the readout says `link: binary frames`). The previous app took it as `tcp.send` commands (hex in JSON, twice the bytes, 64 kB at most), pipelined 8 blocks of 32 kB (`link: pipelined hex`); the oldest one runs commands in a thread pool without order, so the node sends one block of 60 kB at a time and waits for the reply — about 0.3 MB/s over Wi-Fi, i.e. 0.15 MS/s (`link: one block at a time`): update the app. When the link cannot keep up the readout shows *dropped* (whole blocks are dropped, which sounds like stuttering). The path laptop → Wi-Fi → phone → Wi-Fi → laptop crosses the air twice, so a full 1 MS/s wants 5 GHz or a wire; if the SDR and SDR++ are on the same computer, use `node tools/node/bridge.mjs` there (no Wi-Fi at all).
- The protocol is tiny. Server → client on connect, 12 bytes: `52 54 4C 30` (`RTL0`), tuner type (u32, 5 = R820T), gain steps (u32), then an endless stream of uint8 `I Q I Q…` (offset 127.5). Client → server: commands of 5 bytes `[code][u32 big-endian]`: `01` frequency, `02` sample rate, `03` gain mode, `04` gain (tenths of dB), `05` ppm, `06` IF gain, `08` AGC, `09` direct sampling, `0A` offset tuning, `0D` gain index, `0E` bias-T. For example `01 05 F5 E1 00` = tune 100 000 000 Hz. The readout and the `rec` output show each received command as hex with the decoded meaning. `00 00 00 00 00` is our own «hello» from the WebRTC client (the real `rtl_tcp` ignores it): it asks the server for the header.
- **Sample rate.** The client's rate command (`02`) is also output as `sr`, wire it to the SDR. A USB SDR here cannot go below 960 kS/s, so when the client asks for less (SDR++ often asks 250 kS/s) the node **resamples** the stream for it: a low-pass filter (cut 0.47·rate), decimation and a 4-point cubic interpolation to exactly the asked rate (−1 dB at 75 kHz, 300 kHz rejected; the readout says «resampling 1.024 → 0.250 MS/s»). 250 kS/s is also four times less data over Wi-Fi than 1.024 MS/s. If the client asks for slightly more than the stream has (a USB SDR delivers 0.96 MS/s where the client wants 1.024), the node interpolates up (cubic, up to +30 %); for much more the readout warns — raise the SDR rate. When the link is slower than the stream, whole blocks are dropped and counted (*dropped*); the readout line *link:* tells whether the app pipelines the sends (new) or sends one block at a time (old app). About 2 MB/s per 1 MS/s.

Presets: *Remote SDR: FM Receiver (rtl_tcp over WebSocket)*, *Remote SDR: FM Receiver (rtl_tcp over WebRTC)*, *USB SDR: rtl_tcp Server (WebRTC / SDR++)* and *Remote SDR: FM Receiver (SpyServer over WebSocket)*. SoapyRemote and other protocols with their own framing are not supported yet.

### Any pin to the outside (Pins Out / Pins In)

No separate tool: the pins ride on the bridges you already have, with the same protocol. On a computer that is `node tools/node/bridge.mjs` (the one with messengers, serial ports and the CORS proxy; default `ws://127.0.0.1:8777`, `--host 0.0.0.0 --token …` for the network, `wss://` with `--tls`); on a phone it is the DSP app (`ws://phone:8765`) or, in Termux, `node tools/termux/bridge.mjs` (port 8765, `--sources ''` if you only need pins). Put the bridge's address in the node's *url*. The **Pins Out** node (Data) publishes whatever is wired to its inputs under the names from *names*: `iq` (the channel after shift and decimation), `spec` (the spectrum), audio, numbers, text, records. **Pins In** does the opposite: the names are fed from outside and come out on `val` pins, so a number can drive any control (tuning an SDR is `tune → freq`), IQ and spectra go into ordinary IQ / spectrum nodes. Nothing is sent until a client subscribes to a name.

- `websocat -b ws://127.0.0.1:8777/raw/NAME` — only the data: float32 (`sig`, spectrum), float32 `I Q I Q…` (`iq`), UTF-8 JSON (the rest). The centre frequency and rate are in the list: `GET http://127.0.0.1:8777/pins`.
- `ws://…:8777/` — frames with a header (kind, centre, rate, sequence number: see the top of `modules/pins-kernels.js`). `{"t":"sub","names":["chan","spectrum"]}` subscribes (`"*"` — all), `{"t":"set","name":"tune","v":100500000}` writes a number into a Pins In, a binary frame with the same name feeds a stream.
- Another DSP tab (a phone, a second computer) takes the same pins with its own Pins In through the same bridge — a graph split across devices. A slow client loses old frames, nothing piles up. Audio is not resampled: the rates of both engines must match (a warning shows in the node).
- **On the phone.** The bridge of the DSP app (Android) relays pins too, with the same protocol: set the nodes' url to `ws://phone:8765` (token and Origin rules are the app's own; sensors and commands share the port; a computer with `bridge.mjs` is used the same way). `/raw/NAME` and `GET /pins` work there as well. Check: `node tools/test-pins.mjs` (bridge.mjs), `cd tools/android/jvm-test && gradle runPinsTest` (the app).
- **WebRTC instead of the bridge.** Set *transport* to WebRTC and wire *WebRTC Data → rtc* (channel `pins`); the other end is the same Pins node on another device. No bridge and no open port: connect with an offer / answer or the auto-connect room.
- **Pins (custom)** (Data) has no fixed list; the other side creates the pins. List in the parameter *pins*: `>name` — input (graph → out), `<name` — output (in → graph). A bridge client adds a pin by subscribing to a new name, by sending a frame with a new name, or `{"t":"make","name":"tune","dir":"pub"}` (`pub` — the client provides it, `sub` — it wants it). Over WebRTC the nodes announce their names to each other, so a pin made on one device appears on the other; only the wires are yours. *let the other side create pins* off — a fixed list (up to 64 pins).


Presets: *USB SDR: Pins Out / In (channel IQ + spectrum to outside, OpenWebRX-style)* and *Pins (custom): pins made by the other side (WebRTC or bridge)*. Check: `node tools/test-pins.mjs`; in the browser `node tools/e2e-pins.mjs` and `node tools/e2e-pins-custom.mjs` (two pages, bridge and WebRTC).

## tinySA

The **tinySA** node talks to a tinySA or tinySA Ultra over its USB serial console (WebSerial, Chrome/Edge). Wire `spec` to a Spectrum Analyzer to get the trace and a waterfall in dBm.

- **start / stop / points** — sweep range and resolution; `scanraw` (binary, any number of points) is used by default, **transfer format → text** falls back to `scan` (up to 290 / 450 points). Old firmware without `scanraw` is switched to text automatically
- **RBW**, **attenuation**, **spur removal**, **LNA** (Ultra) are sent to the device when changed; **hold** pauses sweeping
- `start` / `stop` inputs (Hz) set the range; `steerFreq` (from `centerFreq` of the Spectrum Analyzer) moves it while keeping the span, so dragging the spectrum pans the tinySA
- outputs: `peakF` / `peakDb` — the highest point of the last sweep
- **Screenshot** reads the device screen (`capture`) to the node and the `img` output, **Save PNG** downloads it
- **gen** (signal generator mode) — `mode low|high output`, frequency, level and **RF on**; `genFreq` (Hz) and `genLevel` (dBm) inputs let the graph drive it (e.g. a stepped frequency sweep)
- ready-made patches: **tinySA: Spectrum**, **tinySA: FPV 5.8 GHz Channel Activity** (Ultra sweep 5.35–5.95 GHz under the FPV channel strip, peak hold, strongest peak on a display)

## Telemetry

The **Telemetry (MAVLink / CRSF / MSP / LTM)** node (Sources) decodes drone telemetry from a serial port (WebSerial, Chrome/Edge): a flight controller over USB, a telemetry radio modem (SiK 433 / 868 / 915 MHz), or the UART of an ELRS / Crossfire receiver. It runs four decoders on the same byte stream; a frame is accepted only with a valid checksum, so *protocol → auto* needs no setting except the baud.

- **MAVLink** v1 / v2 (ArduPilot, PX4): HEARTBEAT (armed), SYS_STATUS (battery), GPS_RAW_INT, ATTITUDE, GLOBAL_POSITION_INT, VFR_HUD. **CRSF** (ELRS, Crossfire; 420000 baud): GPS, battery, attitude, link statistics (RSSI, LQ, SNR), flight mode. **MSP** v1 / v2 (Betaflight, INAV; it only answers requests, so the node asks for attitude, GPS, analog and altitude five times a second — *MSP: ask* switches this off). **LTM** (INAV, Ardupilot through a modem): GPS, attitude, status
- outputs: `lat`, `lon`, `alt` (m), `az` (heading, °), `el` (pitch, °), `roll`, `speed` (m/s), `volts`, `amps`, `rssi`, `lq`, `sats`, `batt` (%), `mode`, and `rec` — a map record (name from *name on the map*, heading and speed for the *Map*); the first six go straight to *Video Overlay* (camera `lat` / `lon` / `alt` / `az` / `el` / `roll`), the rest to its HUD inputs `a`…`d`
- input `text` takes the same bytes as hex, so a log or a test frame can be fed without hardware. Baud: MAVLink 57600 / 115200, MSP 115200, CRSF 420000, LTM 2400–19200
- the numbers are the drone's, not the camera's; the Overlay's correction sliders compensate a tilted camera. Reads only: nothing is sent except the MSP requests
- presets: **Telemetry: Decode Frames (no hardware)**, **Telemetry: HUD over Video → Record** (telemetry → Video Overlay over a video file → Record Video); the parsers are checked by `node tools/test-telemetry.mjs` (checksums against the CRC catalogue check values, frames built by hand from the protocol layouts, not recorded from real drones)

## Remote ID

The **Remote ID (Open Drone ID)** node (Sources) shows drones that broadcast Remote ID (ASTM F3411): ID, type, status, position, altitude, speed, heading and the operator position. The signal is public and unencrypted: Wi-Fi beacons and NAN frames (2.4 / 5 GHz) and Bluetooth advertisements (service data 0xFFFA). Only drones that transmit it are seen (most DJI models and drones made for the EU / US market; home-built FPV usually not). Receive-only.

- hardware: an **ESP32-C5** (2.4 + 5 GHz Wi-Fi and BLE) with the firmware in `tools/odid-esp32c5` (Arduino-ESP32 3.x; not tested on hardware). It hops Wi-Fi channels, scans BLE and prints one line per frame: `ODID,<wifi|ble>,<rssi>,<mac>,<hex>`. **Connect ESP32** opens the port over WebSerial (Chrome/Edge)
- inputs: `text` — the same lines (or bare hex of a message / Message Pack), `rec` — records with `raw` (and optionally `mac`, `rssi`), `lat` / `lon` — your position for the distance (otherwise *My Position*)
- outputs: `rec` — a drone record (`lat`, `lon`, `heading`, `speed`, `alt_m`, `dist_km`, …) and, with *also output the operator position*, an operator record, both for the *Map*; `count`, `frames`, `dist` — the distance to the nearest drone, km
- messages are merged per transmitter address: Basic ID comes every few seconds, Location every second
- presets: **Remote ID: Drones on a Map (ESP32)**, **Remote ID: Demo Frame (no hardware)**; the decoder is checked by `node tools/test-odid.mjs` (vectors built by hand from the standard's layout, not recorded from real drones)

## ESP-SDR

The **ESP-SDR (ESP32)** node talks to an ESP32 running the [ESPARGOS/esp-sdr](https://github.com/ESPARGOS/esp-sdr) firmware (WebSerial, Chrome/Edge): the chip's undocumented debug path gives raw I/Q of the built-in 2.4 / 5 GHz radio, no extra SDR hardware. Wire `spec` to a Spectrum Analyzer.

- **mode → fft** (default): the chip computes the FFT itself (`SPEC`, profiles from `SPECINFO?`) and streams CRC-checked frames, so the spectrum is continuous at tens of frames per second; **sample rate** / **FFT size** must match a profile of the chip, **detector** is mean or max power. Level — dB re full scale
- **mode → burst**: `CAP16` snapshots (signed 8-bit I/Q, CRC32), the FFT runs in the browser; bursts are short, so signals between them are missed. Used when the firmware has no `SPEC`
- **frequency** (100–6000 MHz, 1 MHz steps; the chip really tunes only near its band), **analog bandwidth**, **hold**; changing a setting restarts the stream. **fftshift** (advanced, on by default) reorders the on-chip bins so DC sits in the centre; turn it off if a firmware already sends them centred
- `freq` input (Hz) and `steerFreq` (from the Spectrum Analyzer's `centerFreq`) retune the chip; outputs `peakF` / `peakDb`
- not supported yet: 10-bit `CAP20`, continuous `IQS` stream, gain control (hardware AGC stays on)
- ready-made patch: **ESP-SDR: ESP32 Spectrum**
