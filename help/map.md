# Map and records

Map, records and turning decoded traffic into graphs of entities.

## Map and records

**Records** (`rec` port) carry objects with arbitrary fields — `{lat, lon, id, snr, …}` — from decoders, CSV files and sensors to the map, logs and filters.

- **Fields → Rec** builds a record from its inputs (the field list is editable, ports appear on *Apply fields*); constants like `icon=plane; color=#f80` are added to every record. With the `rec` input it adds/overrides fields in passing records. Emits on change, on a `go` trigger, or every block.
- **Rec → Fields** splits the last record back into ports; *Fields from last rec* fills the list from what actually arrives.
- **CSV → Rec** (text lines or a whole file, header or explicit field names, `,` `;` tab), **Rec Log** (save CSV / GeoJSON / KML / GPX, replay; the map and *Rec: Unique by Key* export the same way), **Rec Filter** (JS condition over `r`).
- **NMEA Parser** (Data): NMEA 0183 text from a GNSS receiver, echo sounder, anemometer, compass or an AIS feed → records and numbers (see [NMEA 0183](#nmea-0183)).
- **My Position**: typed in (lat/lon or Maidenhead locator, ⌖ fills it once from geolocation), live GPS of the browser, or *wire* — `lat` / `lon` (and `acc` `alt` `speed` `heading`) from the inputs, e.g. from **Phone sensors** (a position without the browser's geolocation; Satellites, ADS-B and the other nodes that take the shared position follow it). Preset *My Position from Phone Sensors (wire) → Satellites and Map*, test `node tools/test-geome-wire.mjs`. Other nodes use it for distances and bearings. *look direction* (used when GPS has no heading), *field of view*, its range and *distance rings* go into the record, so the Map draws your own view sector and rings.
- **Geo from Text**: coordinates from any decoded text — degrees/minutes/seconds, NMEA and APRS (`4903.50N/07201.75W`), ACARS (`N55123E037456`), decimal pairs, 6-char locators (4-char optional).
- **Mark Point**: your position + RSSI/SNR/azimuth/frequency as a record, on a button, a trigger (`go`, e.g. a Joystick button) or every N seconds; **📷 Mark + photo** opens the camera (the phone's camera or the gallery) and puts the photo into the record. The record is a field measurement: `lat lon alt` (from the wires or My Position), `h` — receiving antenna height above ground (the *antenna height* parameter), `rssi` / `snr`, `azimuth`, `freq`, `tx` — the source being measured (Source Locator groups by it), `session` — *field* (moving about) or *quiet* (a calm place for a long listen: filter a Table by `session` to feed one or the other set), `rx_ant`, `rx_gain`, `note`, `photo`, `name` (P1, P2…). Wire `rec` to a Table (store, edit later), to Source Locator and to the Map. The level can be typed in the *level by hand, dBm* parameter (used while the `rssi` input is not wired). Presets *Field Survey: Mark Points with Photos → Table, Locator, Map* and *Trilateration: Type RSSI, Phone GPS → Locator, Map* (phone GPS + typed RSSI → Source Locator → Map).
- **Source Locator**: finds a transmitter from marks (or many at once with *group by*, e.g. `bssid` — one estimate per access point) — signal level (log-distance model, unknown power) and/or bearings; outputs the estimate with an error radius and shows the probability map. Level-based location works on VHF/UHF at short range; on HF (skywave) only bearings make sense. **Whole list from a Table**: the `rows` input takes the Table's list at once (with its filter; marks in the rows need `lat lon` / `grid` and a level or `azimuth`). The set is cached by row — when it changes only the added or deleted rows are applied and only the affected sources (groups) are recomputed; a deleted row disappears from the estimate. **Position accuracy**: a mark with `acc` (m, as the phone GPS gives it) is weighted by it — the position error shifts the level by 4.34·n·acc/d dB, so a poor fix hurts most near the source (parameter *weigh marks by position accuracy*, on by default). Rows from a Table are not trimmed by *keep measurements*; *Clear* empties the node and the list is loaded again. **Height (3D)**: marks may carry `elevation` (° above the horizon; *Mark Point* has an `elevation` input) — every mark with it gives the source altitude `z + d·tan(el)` (weighted: near marks and flat angles count more; *elevation error*), and the estimate gets `alt`, `h` (above ground, from the Horizon map), `alt_err`, `height_src` (`elevation` / `assumed` — ground at the point + *assumed source height*). The `alt` output and `rec` go to Video Overlay (a diamond at the real height), the Map or a Table; `rays` (*rays* parameter) are links from every mark (its `alt`, or ground + `h`) to the estimate — wire to Overlay `rec2` and the Map to see the bearing lines in 3D; *Save estimates to table* → `analysis/sources` (`lat lon alt h err_m alt_err_m height_src`). Logic: `modules/locate3d-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-locate3d.mjs`.
- **FT8** now outputs decoded messages (`msg`) and records: call (`from`), addressee (`to`), locator (remembered per call), SNR (2500 Hz, WSJT-X style estimate), distance and azimuth from your position — a propagation map in one wire.
- **Station Schedule** (Radio): EiBi CSV or your own CSV (`khz, time, days, station, lang, target, itu, lat, lon`). Stations on air now go to the spectrum (`bands` → Spectrum Analyzer), the ones on the tuned frequency to the `now` text, transmitters to the map (country centre when the file has no coordinates; EiBi `/XXX` relay sites are used). Download by URL (optional CORS proxy — eibispace.de has no CORS headers) or load the file; it is kept in the browser. Filters: *only aimed at my area* (EiBi target codes — `Eu`, `EEu`, `NAm`, `As`… — worked out from your position, or type your own list, country codes like `RUS` included), *max distance to transmitter*, and `spanLo`/`spanHi` inputs (wire `freqLo`/`freqHi` from USB SDR) to label only what the receiver sees.

**Map** fields: `lat`, `lon` (or `grid` — Maidenhead locator), `id` (same id → one object with a track), `t`, `label`, `icon` (`dot square triangle diamond star cross plus plane ship car truck bus train tram bike moto heli drone walk antenna tx rx me flag sat balloon` or any emoji/text; vehicles turn with `heading` or along the track), `color`, `size`, `radius` (m, circle), `azimuth` + `range` (km, great-circle bearing line), `rings` (distance rings around the object, km: `1,5,10` or one number, labelled), `fov` (field of view, °) + `fov_range` (km; default `range`, else the largest ring) — a sector around `heading` (or `azimuth`, or the track direction), `heading` (rotates plane/triangle; otherwise taken from the track), `lat2`/`lon2` (line to a second point), `path` (`[[lat,lon],…]` ahead), `track: 0` (no history line), `snr`/`rssi` (colour when no `color`). Everything else is shown as `key: value` on click. Tap on an empty spot — coordinates and locator on the `pick` output.

**Editing on the map** (*mode*): *view* (default) only shows; *edit* — tap a point from `rows` (or one added here) and a form opens with its fields (**＋** adds a field, **Save** applies), the selected point can be dragged; *add* — a tap on an empty spot creates a point named `P1`, `P2`… (*new point name prefix*, *new point icon*). *line*, *polygon*, *ruler*, *circle* — drawing modes: a tap adds a vertex, the panel at the bottom left shows the count, length and area while you draw (**↶** removes the last vertex, **✓** finishes, **✕** cancels); a circle is two taps (centre, a point on the edge). A shape is a record too: `gtype` (`line` / `polygon` / `ruler` / `circle`), `geom` (`[[lat,lon],…]` as JSON text; a circle is `lat`/`lon` + `radius`), `lat`/`lon` — the centre, and the measurements `length_m` (perimeter for a polygon), `area_m2`; `color` is set by *color of new shapes*. A ruler also labels every segment with its length and bearing and the total. Lengths and areas are great-circle / spherical; drag in *edit* moves the whole shape. Shapes go to GeoJSON / KML as LineString / Polygon. **Editing a shape** (*edit*): tap its body (inside a polygon / circle, next to a line) to select it — squares appear on the vertices and small circles in the middle of the edges. Drag a vertex to move it; press a middle circle to insert a vertex and drag it at once; **double tap** a vertex to delete it (a polygon keeps at least 3, a line 2); a circle has a diamond on its edge to change the radius; dragging the centre marker moves the whole shape. Length, area and the centre are recalculated and go to the Table on release. In *edit*, **🗑** in the form deletes the point or shape: a record `{name, _delete: 1}` goes to `edit`, and a Table with *upsert key col* removes the row with that name.

**How to use it**: tick *mode buttons on the map* (Edit group) and a row of buttons appears over the map — ☝ view, ✎ edit, ＋ add a point, ╱ line, ⬠ polygon, 📏 ruler, ◯ circle — so a tile without the settings panel can switch modes too. In ✎ a **long press on an empty spot** puts a new point (`P1`, `P2`…) and opens its form, so adding and editing need no mode switch; ＋ keeps adding point after point; after a shape is finished the map goes back to ✎. The form has the **name** (edit it and **Save** to rename — links to the record follow), **▾** next to *icon* opens a grid of icons (the button at the end of the toolbar chooses the icon of new points; the grid opens next to the button you pressed), **＋** adds a field, 🗑 deletes; the checklist of a point is at the top of the form. *icon size ×* (Display) scales every icon and the checklist badge; the checklist items sit at the top of the form.

**Links between points**: the ⤳ tool — tap one point, then another and a link appears (the same pair again removes it); in ✎ a tap on a line opens its form (label, weight, colour, dashed, 🗑). Links are records `id, from, to, label, weight, color, dash` (the ends are the names of the points, as in Graph) and go to the `link` output — wire it to a **second Table** (a list of its own, *upsert key col* `id`) and wire that Table's `rows` back to the Map's `links` input (links can also arrive in `rows`, like in Graph). Deleting or renaming a point deletes / renames its links in that Table. **Snapping**: a vertex of a line, polygon or ruler that is put (or dragged) within a few pixels of a point lands exactly on it.

Preset **Map Editor: Points, Shapes, Rings, Checklists ↔ Table** shows it all on a small data set (shops with checklists, a car with a view sector, a zone, a route, a circle, your own sector) that it puts into the Table lists `demo/trip` and `demo/trip tasks`.

Every change goes to the `edit` output as a record `{name, lat, lon, …changed fields}` and shows on the map at once. Wire `edit` to a [Table](data.md#table) `rec` input, set the Table's *upsert key col* to `name` (the column the points are named by) and wire its `rows` back to the map: new points become rows, edits and moves update them.

Works offline: the vector base map (Natural Earth 10m: coast, lakes, rivers, country and region borders, ~7 000 cities, English names) is fetched once from `data/basemap.json` and kept in IndexedDB. *Download places* adds ~130 000 towns from GeoNames (shown from zoom 8), or import a GeoNames dump / CSV `name,lat,lon[,population]`. Optional OSM / OpenTopoMap tiles are cached as you view them and stay available offline. Points can be saved in the browser under a name (*save points as*). Rebuild the base map: `node tools/basemap.mjs`.

- **Satellites** (Radio): SGP4 orbits from CelesTrak TLE groups (amateur, weather, NOAA, stations…) and uplink/downlink frequencies from the SatNOGS database — both downloaded once and kept in the browser (or imported from files; set a CORS proxy if a site refuses cross-origin requests). On the map: position, visibility footprint, ground track ahead; click a satellite to select it. For the selected one: az/el/range, next passes (24 h), sky plot, and **Doppler**: `freq` is the downlink as heard (wire it to `tuneFreq` of the USB SDR — the channel follows the pass), `up` is what to transmit. Downlinks of satellites above the horizon are marked on the spectrum (`bands`). Orbits: [satellite.js](https://github.com/shashwatak/satellite-js) 5.0.0 (MIT) in `vendor/`. TLEs age: refresh them every week or two.
- **Horizon** (Radio): the skyline with mountains as an `azimuth → elevation` profile for the observer (*My Position* or wired `lat` / `lon`). Heights come from Terrain-RGB tiles (AWS Terrarium, one per ~40 km at zoom 10, downloaded once and kept in the browser); every azimuth is traced out to *radius* with earth curvature and refraction (*k*, 4/3 by default) from the antenna height. `el` is the horizon at the `az` input (+ *margin*), `max` / `maxaz` is the highest point. The profile is recomputed when the point moves by more than 1 km. Or import your own `azimuth, elevation` file (CSV / TXT, gaps are interpolated) — for buildings and trees, which the terrain data does not have. Open data resolves ~30 m, zoom 10 is ~150 m per pixel: lower the zoom for speed, raise it for nearby ridges. Set a CORS proxy if the tile server refuses cross-origin requests. **Offline**: every tile is kept in the browser database (IndexedDB) on the first download and never fetched again; decoded tiles stay in memory (up to 240), so a window that follows a moving observer decodes only the new ones. *Download area for offline* (advanced) fetches a circle of *download area* km around the observer at the chosen zoom in advance (up to 2500 tiles) and asks the browser for persistent storage, so the data is not cleared when space runs low.
- **Radio Reach** (Radio): how far you receive aircraft from a point with a given antenna, with the mountains in the way. For every azimuth it walks the terrain of the *Horizon* height map (100 m steps, then 1 km beyond the map, where the ground is taken as the observer's ground or sea level — *far*) with the curvature of the Earth and refraction *k*, and finds where an aircraft at the given altitude goes below the line over the highest terrain in front of it — the first loss of the signal. **Several antennas (*ants*, m above ground: `2, 10, 30`) and altitudes (*alts*: `FL100, FL250`, feet or `3000m`) at once**: a polar plot with a curve per altitude for the antenna picked by *antNo*, a readout with min (and its azimuth) / avg / max reach and the covered area for every pair, and a line comparing the antennas at the picked altitude — test antenna heights and compare places (move the point, or feed `lat` / `lon` / `h` from a *Table* row, and log the results). Check: on flat ground the reach of a 10 m antenna at FL100 is 237 km (the radio horizon, 4.12 km·(√10+√2948)). **Received aircraft**: wire the *ADS-B Decoder* `rec` — every aircraft is a dot on the plot, the dashed line is the farthest received per 5° sector, `seen` / `obs` count them, and a received aircraft that the calculation says is hidden is red (`miss`): tune *k* and the antenna height until the red dots go away. **Out**: `min` `avg` `max` (km) and `area` (km²) for the picked pair; `rec` — a summary record per pair (point, ground, antenna, altitude, min / avg / max, area, worst and best azimuth) once per calculation (wire it to a *Table* to keep a log); `poly` — the coverage areas as records with a `path` for the **Map**. *Save to table* appends the summary to the list in *save* (`analysis/reach`). The point must lie inside the radius of a *Horizon* node (the terrain is downloaded there); without Horizon a flat Earth is assumed. Preset *Radio Reach: ADS-B Coverage over Terrain (Generator)*. Logic: `modules/reach-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-reach.mjs`.
- **Ambient Light** (Sources): illuminance in lux from `AmbientLightSensor` (`lux` number, `slux` signal; Chrome / Android, may need the flag `#enable-generic-sensor-extra-classes`)
- **Sensor (Generic Sensor API)** also reads `GravitySensor` (gravity without the motion; in Firefox / Safari it is taken from `devicemotion`) and `RelativeOrientationSensor` (quaternion `qx qy qz qw` without the compass)
- **Compute Pressure** (Sources): CPU load state of the device (`nominal` / `fair` / `serious` / `critical`) as `level` 0…3, `state` and a `rec` record on every change (Chrome / Edge 125+)
- **Network Information** (Sources): `downlink` (Mbit/s), `rtt` (ms), `online` and the connection `type`, a `rec` on every change (Chrome / Edge)
- **Idle Detection** (Sources): `idle` (user away), `locked` (screen locked) and `state`; press *Allow and start*, the threshold is at least 60 s (Chrome / Edge)
- **Broadcast Channel** (Sources): messages between tabs and windows of the same site by a channel name — the `send` / `val` inputs go out, the messages of other tabs (and other nodes with the same name) come on `text` / `rec`
- **WebTransport** (Sources): datagrams over HTTP/3 (QUIC) — `send` out, `text` / `rec` in; for a self-signed server (14 days at most) put its certificate SHA-256 into *certificate SHA-256*
- **Camera**: *torch* (flash light) and *Take photo* — a full-resolution still through `ImageCapture`, saved as a JPEG file; *Record video* (the `rec` input starts it by level) writes the camera track as webm / mp4, with the microphone sound if *record: with sound* is on — see [Camera tools](video.md#camera-tools)
- **Orientation** (Sources): where a phone's camera looks — `az` (0 = north, clockwise), `el` (above the horizon) and `roll`, from `AbsoluteOrientationSensor` (Chrome / Android) or `deviceorientation` (Safari asks for permission; press *Start*). *Camera* picks the rear or the front lens, the screen rotation is accounted for. The compass of a phone is off by 5–15°: correct it in *Video Overlay*.
- **Video Overlay** (Video): a picture (`vid` — the new full-size video output of *Camera* and *Video (file/URL)*; or an `img` raster — *TV Decoder* of an analog FPV / TV signal, SSTV, LRPT) with objects and a HUD over it. It reads no sensors itself: the camera position (`lat` / `lon` / `alt`, m — *My Position*) and direction (`az` / `el` / `roll` — *Orientation*, or telemetry of a drone) come on its inputs, `fov` is a parameter or an input. Objects come on `rec` / `rec2` (*Satellites*, *ADS-B Decoder*, AIS, any record with `lat` / `lon` and an altitude — `alt_km`, `alt_m`, `alt` in metres, ADS-B `alt` in feet — or just `az` / `el` / `range_km`) and are projected from their coordinates on the WGS84 ellipsoid: stub wire models (aircraft, satellite, ship, other), labels, `path` of a satellite, ADS-B extrapolated along the course. Also drawn: the horizon with compass marks, an elevation ladder, the skyline from *Horizon*, the **terrain relief in 3D** (*relief*: a shaded mesh over the heights of *Horizon* out to its *radius* — snow, rock, grass by height, light by slope, haze with distance; *lines* — a wire mesh over a video; *auto* — solid without a video; an aircraft behind a ridge is dimmed; without an `alt` input the camera stands on the ground by the height map; add a *Horizon* node to the patch, it downloads the tiles) and the HUD — crosshair, a heading tape, numbers from inputs `a`…`d`. *find* puts an arrow at the edge to an object outside the frame. Without a video it draws the sky. Presets *AR: Satellites Through the Camera (Phone)*, *AR: ADS-B Aircraft in the Sky*, *AR: Drones Around (Remote ID, Phone)* and *Telemetry: HUD over Video → Record*. The output `vid` is the finished picture (video, objects and HUD) — wire it to *Record Video*. **More**: *Sun and Moon* are drawn at their true positions and sizes (the Moon with its phase and the bright limb towards the Sun); **calibration** — put the crosshair on the Sun, the Moon, a tapped object or a *find* match and press *Align crosshair to target*: the azimuth / elevation corrections are set for you (a phone compass is off by 5–15°; the Sun, the Moon, planets and stars are the reference that needs no network). Also drawn: **planets** (Mercury … Neptune, Keplerian elements, an arc-minute or so), **bright stars** up to a chosen magnitude (46 of them: Sirius, Vega, Polaris, the Big Dipper, the Southern Cross…) and **deep sky** (Andromeda, Pleiades, Orion Nebula, Magellanic Clouds, Omega Centauri, the Galactic Centre) — tap one to select it or align to it; *find* matches their names. **Tap** an object: a card (name, range, azimuth / elevation, altitude, speed, heading, LOS), an arrow when it is off screen, and the `pick` record plus `paz` / `pel` / `prange` outputs. **Line of sight**: every object is tested against the terrain rings of *Horizon* — an aircraft or a satellite behind a ridge is dimmed and marked “no LOS”; `vis` is the record list of what is in line of sight (feed it to a Table or a scanner), `nvis` / `nhid` the counts. **Trails** of moving objects (*trails, s*, the positions kept as they arrive, older parts fade). **Video delay** (*lag*, ms): the pose is taken from that long ago, so the marks do not run ahead of a delayed picture. **Drone**: *tilt* is the camera's angle relative to the heading / pitch input (FPV cameras sit tilted up), *home* (first fix or *Set home here*) draws a flag, the distance, the bearing and an arrow; Remote ID drones get a quadcopter stub and their `height_m` is counted from the terrain. **OSM** (*OSM*, off by default): peaks with heights, towns, major roads and rivers from Overpass for *OSM radius* around the camera (cached in the browser; a feature behind the relief is not drawn; Horizon heights are needed to lay them on the ground). **Airfields and towns** (*airfields and runways*, *towns and villages*, off by default): aerodromes, helipads, runways (drawn to scale, not thinner than 3 px, with the designator «09/27») and towns / villages with the distance, laid on the Horizon terrain. **Points live in Table lists**: everything is written to the lists `geo/airfields`, `geo/runways`, `geo/places` and `geo/peaks` of the [Table](data.md#table) database (only missing rows are added, so your edits stay) and *Video Overlay*, *Flight Sim* and the map read the lists, not a private cache; *own points* (`lists`: one or more Table lists, comma-separated; columns `name`, `lat`, `lon`, `h` — height above ground in m, or `alt` above sea level, `color`) are drawn as coloured diamonds with the distance — signal sources, observation posts, masts. Airfields come from the **OurAirports** database (two CSV files, ~14 MB, downloaded once and kept in the browser; no Overpass needed), or from OSM cells if it cannot be downloaded; towns — at once from the built-in map (and from the GeoNames list if the *Map* node downloaded it), plus villages from OSM Overpass per 0.5° cell (each cell is kept in the browser; a busy server — HTTP 5xx, a «runtime error» answer — is never cached, the next mirror is tried, then a minute's pause). Everything is downloaded apart from the terrain; *Download area for offline* fetches the airport database and a circle of OSM cells of *download area radius* km in advance. **The Sun** is the real one for the place and the time: the terrain mesh is lit by it (slopes towards the Sun are brighter), casts shadows (*shadows*: rays towards the Sun over the heights, to 12 km, a few ms when the mesh is rebuilt), turns dark at night, and without a video the sky goes from night to day blue (stars and planets only when the Sun is down); *haze: visibility* fades distant ridges into the sky (clearer air high on the slopes); the terrain is lit from smoothed vertex normals (no facets); the visible horizon (the compass line) is lower than the level by the dip of the horizon at the altitude (≈1.5° at 2500 m) and the land beyond the mesh radius is filled in the haze colour up to it; the Horizon skyline (orange) is drawn only where it is valid — at the height it was computed for and when the 3D relief is not drawn. *Sun time shift* moves the hour (+6 h — evening shadows), the Sun and its azimuth / elevation are on the status line. Logic: `modules/geofeat-kernels.js`, `modules/geofeat.js`, `node tools/test-geofeat.mjs`. The geometry is in `overlay-kernels.js`, checked by `node tools/test-overlay.mjs` (the Sun and the Moon against the Meeus examples). Record fields for the stub models: `heading`, `roll` and `pitch` (°, a banking and climbing aircraft); `gone: true` with an `id` removes the object at once. The terrain mesh is built once and shifted while the camera moves less than 60 m, so a flying camera does not rebuild it every frame.
- **Flight Sim** (Sources): an arcade aircraft and cameras for the *Video Overlay* — a minimal flight simulator and a viewer for ADS-B traffic. **fly**: your own aircraft on `ail` / `elev` / `rud` (−1…1) and `thr` (a stick −1…1 with the centre at half throttle, a lever 0…1, or *stick up only* where the lower half is idle — the *Joystick* with *hold = left Y* starts at idle), banking turns, a stall at low speed (the nose drops until the speed returns), take-off and landing; a hard touchdown, a fast one or a bank over 25° is a crash (*Reset* or a pulse on `reset`). It is a simple kinematic model (not aerodynamics): the stick sets the bank and the pitch angle, the throttle sets the speed. The start point is the `lat` / `lon` inputs, else *My Position*, else the advanced parameters; *start = nearest runway* puts the aircraft on the nearest OSM runway within 30 km (the same airfield cells as in *Video Overlay*, downloaded once), heading along it, throttle idle — take off with 38+ m/s and the stick pulled back; the aircraft waits (up to 20 s) until *Horizon* knows the ground there, then starts *start height* above it. **watch ADS-B**: the camera follows one aircraft of the `rec` input (*ADS-B Decoder*): the nearest to the tower, or the one matching *follow* (a callsign or an ICAO code), *Next aircraft* (or a pulse on `next`) switches; positions are extrapolated along the course and the bank is estimated from the turn rate. **Cameras** (`cam`, a pulse on the `cam` input switches): *chase* (behind and above, the heading lags a turn), *cockpit*, *tower* (a fixed point, looks at the aircraft and zooms on it) and *orbit* around it. Outputs `lat lon alt az el roll fov` are the camera — wire them to *Video Overlay* and to *Horizon* (the terrain follows the camera); `rec` is your aircraft as a stub model with its bank and pitch (hidden in the cockpit view); `kts agl vsi hdg pwr` go to the HUD inputs `a…d`. The ground is `Horizon.hAt` (without *Horizon* a flat ground at *ground height*). The engine must be running: the time goes by its blocks. Presets *Flight Sim: Fly over Terrain (Joystick)* and *Flight Sim: Watch ADS-B Aircraft (Generator)*. Logic: `modules/flightsim-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-flightsim.mjs`.
- **Record Video** (Output): the mirror of *Video (file/URL)* — frames from `vid` (a video, a canvas, the `vid` output of *Video Overlay* with its HUD) or `img` (*TV Decoder*, *TV Hopper* mosaic, SSTV…) go to a webm / mp4 file through MediaRecorder at a constant frame rate. *Record / stop* and **Snapshot PNG**; *format* (auto — the first the browser can encode: VP9, VP8, H.264), *frame rate*, *bitrate*, *max width*, *save to* — a download or **a file on disk** (streamed, no length limit; Chrome / Edge). Inputs: `gate` — record while it is above 0 (a decoder `lock`, a race flag…), `snap` — a PNG on its rising edge; outputs `on`, `sec`. A webm from MediaRecorder has no duration in its header: browsers and VLC play it, some editors want a remux (`ffmpeg -i in.webm -c copy out.webm`). Presets *Video: File with HUD → Record*, *Analog TV: Record Video and Snapshots (Generator)*

- **Internet Radio** (Sources): search [radio-browser.info](https://www.radio-browser.info) — an open community database with a public API — by name, tag and country; the result is kept in the browser. Stations go out on `rows` as one set — wire it to the Map `rows` and a new search replaces the points (stations without coordinates sit around the country centre); wire the same `rows` to a Table and the list of stations is stored there (*rows wire*: replace or add); click one to play it: its URL goes to *Audio Stream (URL)*, which now starts on a new URL from the wire and gives the sound in stereo (`L` / `R`; a mono stream is the same in both; *Capture Tab/Screen (Audio)* is stereo too; old patches with `audio` are converted on load). An https page cannot play `http://` streams, and only streams that send CORS headers can be captured into the graph.

### Rec → Entities rules

One rule per line, `#` starts a comment. The separator is a space-bar-space ` | ` (a bare `|` inside `a|b` means "or").

```
[out[!]:] conditions | fields
```

**Conditions** — space-separated, all must match:

| Form | Matches when |
|---|---|
| `field` | the field has a value |
| `!field` | the field is missing or empty |
| `field=a\|b` | the value equals `a` or `b` |
| `field!=a` | the value differs from `a` |
| `field~regex` | the value matches the regex (case-insensitive) |

**Fields** — comma-separated:

| Form | Result |
|---|---|
| `name=template` | a field built from the template |
| `name` | copy of the record field, if present |
| `*` | all fields of the record |
| `name=` (empty template) | an empty column, e.g. `note=` for your own comments |

**Template** — `{f}` a record field · `{a\|b}` the first non-empty of `a`, `b` · `{f:x}` a number as hex · `{f*}` an array field, one output record per element. If a template needs a field the record lacks, the rule is skipped for that record. `t` is carried over by itself.

**Output** — no prefix: the result goes to `rec` only. `out:` — to the `out` port and to `rec`. `out!:` — to the `out` port only. Press *Apply outputs* to create the ports. `count` is reserved.

Examples (records are made up):

```
# {proto:'ble', src:'aa:bb', name:'Band', rssi:-60}  →  port ble: {id:'aa:bb', type:'ble', label:'Band', rssi:-60}
ble: proto=ble src | id={src}, type=ble, label={name|src}, rssi

# one frame → a node and a link, each to its own port only
node!: proto=wifi src | id={src}, type=station, label={src}
link!: proto=wifi src bssid | id={src}>{bssid}, from={src}, to={bssid}, label={kind}

# {id:'250-01-5', cellArfcns:[10,20]}  →  two records: ARFCN 10, ARFCN 20
arfcn: cellArfcns | id=ARFCN {cellArfcns*}, label=ch {cellArfcns*}

# only beacons and probe responses
ap!: proto=wifi kind=beacon|probe-resp bssid | id={bssid}, type=ap, label={ssid|bssid}

# only broadcast frames
bc!: proto=wifi dst=ff:ff:ff:ff:ff:ff | id={dst}, type=broadcast
```

**Grouping and counting** is not done by the node: it only turns records into records. Put a **Table** with *upsert key col* = `id` (or **Rec: Unique by Key**) after the port — a repeated `id` updates its row and keeps `count`, `first` and `last`. What you put into `id` is the grouping: `{src}` — per device, `ch{chan}` — per channel, `{src}>{dst}` — per pair. Sums and averages need another node.

- **Geotag** (Geo; `modules/geotag.js`, core `geotag-kernels.js`, `node tools/test-geotag.mjs`): glues a position to anything. Records on `rec` (packets, finds of a decoder, measurements) and the number on `in` (a level, a temperature: one record `{t, <field>, …}` every *every* seconds, field *rssi* by default) come out on `rec` with `lat`, `lon`, `acc`, `alt`, `speed`, `gage` (age of the fix, s) and `gfix` (`interp` / `last` / `first`). The position is taken at the **time of the record** (field `t`, ms or s; without it — arrival; *position at the time of: arrival* switches it off): between two fixes it is interpolated, so a record that a decoder delivers late still lands where it was received. A fix older than *maxAge* is not used: *no position* drops the record or passes it on untagged. Fixes come from records with `lat` / `lon` on `pos` (Phone Sensors, Mock GPS, any GPS node), numbers on `lat` / `lon` (`acc`, `alt`), or — when nothing is wired and *own* is on — the GPS of this device. *step* (m) and *dt* (s) thin the records out, *constants* (`dev=sdr1; band=433`) go into every record, the record's own `lat` / `lon` win unless *overwrite* is on. Outputs: `rec`, `count`, `ok`, `lat`, `lon`, `acc`, `age`, `dist` (walked, m). The Map takes the records straight on `rec`, and through a Table on `meas` it draws the survey (track coloured by level, fields `dev`, `lat`, `lon`, `rssi`, `t`); Source Locator takes them too.
