# Analysis

Recognize signals, measure them, watch spectra and logic.

## Signal type identifier

The **Signal Type Identifier** (`sigid`) node labels every signal it finds and suggests a decoder for it. It doesn't care where the signal comes from:

- `spec`: a spectrum from any node (USB SDR, FFT, tinySA…). The node finds the signals and classifies them by shape: bandwidth (99% power), flat top, carrier over the sidebands, which sideband holds the energy, and on/off keying over time
- USB SDR: the node also reads the **raw IQ at the native sample rate** that comes with the spectrum. It takes one signal at a time, shifts it to zero, decimates it and measures the envelope, the instantaneous frequency, the lines in z, z² and z⁴, and the keying rate. The heavy part runs in a Web Worker
- `in`: audio (microphone, KiwiSDR, a receiver's output). Without `spec` the node computes its own spectrum, and it analyses the audio samples the same way
- **whole spectrum** / **search range**: limit where signals are looked for. The `fmin` / `fmax` inputs set the range too, e.g. from a Table's `lo` / `hi` (a band plan list), so clicking a band limits the search to that band. A new value on these inputs unticks **whole spectrum**
- `plan`: a band plan for context. Its label is shown next to each signal, and a matching mode adds some confidence

What it recognises: carrier, CW (with WPM), OOK, AM, USB/LSB, NFM (with CTCSS tone), WFM (with the stereo pilot), 2-FSK / 4-FSK with shift and baud (RTTY, AFSK 1200 / APRS, POCSAG, DMR/P25-like 4800 Bd), MFSK (FT8-like, Olivia/Contestia-like), BPSK / QPSK with symbol rate (PSK31…), OFDM, DTMF keys. Each result says whether it came from the spectrum or from the samples. It is a heuristic, not a decoder.

The `bands` output carries the labels. Wire it into a Spectrum Analyzer's `bands`, or into a **Table**'s `sigs` so the labels show together with the bands: each signal gets a bracket at its peak level with its type above it.

Ready-made patches: **USB SDR: Signal Identifier**, **HF: Quick-Decode All Protocols**.

## Signal Analyzer

**Signal Analyzer** (`sigLab`, Analysis) is the node for taking apart a transmission nobody has described to you, in the manner of [Universal Radio Hacker](https://github.com/jopohl/urh): a capture, the demodulated signal with the messages, symbols and bits drawn on it, and a table of the messages in which you mark the fields. It takes an `iq` stream (USB SDR, IQ Generator, a decimator) or a signal (`in`: the output of a receiver, a comparator, a sound card).

- **Capture**: *rolling* keeps the last *capture length* seconds and analyses again every *analyse every* seconds (the work is done in a worker, so the graph does not stall); *single* fills one buffer and stops; *trigger* waits for a level (*trigger level*, dBFS) and stops 85 % of a buffer later, with 15 % before the trigger. **Run / Stop**, **Clear**, **Analyse**; the `go` input restarts a *single* / *trigger* capture on a rising edge. At most *samples kept at most* samples (4 M by default) are kept: decimate a wide IQ stream first.
- **What it finds by itself** (turn *find … by itself* off to set the values by hand, or drag the lines on the plot):
  - **modulation** (*modulation* auto): FSK when the instantaneous frequency of the strong samples has two or four humps, PSK when the carrier inside the pulses is not coherent, otherwise ASK (OOK is ASK). A real `in` signal (sound: AFSK, tones, PSK on a subcarrier) is turned into a complex one with a Hilbert transform first, so FSK and PSK work on audio too; *raw* takes the input as already demodulated data (the NFM audio of an FSK transmission): levels are the bits, the pauses and noise are cut by the noise level (set it by hand when the data is quieter than the noise between packets);
  - **noise level** (the red line): the amplitude between the noise floor and the signal; everything above it is a message, a gap shorter than the *pause* (0 — taken from the gaps: the largest jump between the gaps inside messages and the pauses between them, otherwise 12 symbols) does not end a message;
  - **centre** (the amber line): the decision level of the demodulated signal (Otsu; for four levels k-means with three thresholds);
  - **symbol rate**: run lengths of the binarised signal and the distances between edges of one sign, tested against candidate periods (a smoothing window grows until the estimate stops being noise), refined by a least-squares fit of the edge times to a symbol grid; a message with its own, clearly different rate (*own symbol rate…*) gets its own period, so a capture with several devices works. *baud* / **Lock baud** fix it;
  - symbols are sampled in the middle of each period by a clock that follows the edges; the centre is found per message, so a drifting FSK carrier is not a problem. *symbol levels* 4 gives dibits (*Gray code* if needed), PSK needs the carrier removed: BPSK / QPSK by the M-th power;
  - **line code** (*line code of the bits*, auto by default): NRZ, NRZ-M, NRZ-S, Manchester (both polarities; the pair phase is chosen by the fewest invalid pairs), differential Manchester; *auto* takes Manchester when the raw bits have no run longer than two and the pairs are 01 / 10 in one phase, otherwise NRZ (the other codes are chosen by hand); *invert* flips the bits.
- **The plot**: a magnitude lane (with the noise line) and the demodulated lane (with the centre line, and the thresholds for four levels); each message is a coloured band with its number and length, like bands on a spectrum; when a symbol is wider than a few pixels the cells with the symbol values appear under the curve, with the grid lines across, and for the selected message the decoded bits and the labelled fields. **Wheel** zooms at the cursor, **drag** pans, **Shift + wheel** pans, a pinch zooms on a phone, **double-click** a message zooms to it (on the empty plot — fits all), a click selects a message, the strip at the bottom shows the whole capture (click or drag to move the window). **Shift + drag** measures a span (time and symbols); *it is N symbol(s)* + **set baud** sets the symbol rate from it.
- **The table** (*bin* / *hex* / *ascii*; *bytes: least significant bit first*): one row per message — time, length, bits; with *align = sync* (the *sync word* you type in hex, e.g. `85CFAB45` — it works for a single message too and the preamble stays in front of it — or, when the field is empty, the word found by comparing the messages) the messages are shifted so that the sync word found by comparing them (and the preamble before it: alternating bits) stands in one column, otherwise they start at the left edge. With *table: dim the columns that are the same…* on (off by default), the columns in which all messages agree are dimmed, so the fields that change stand out. The sync word is underlined. A click selects a message; in the selected one drag over bits (bytes in the hex view) and give the field a name: it gets a colour in the table and on the plot, its value (decimal and hex) is shown for the selected message, and it goes into the records. Fields are kept in the patch (with the alignment mode they were drawn in). **copy** puts all messages in the shown format on the clipboard.
- **Splitting a stream** (*cut a long message into frames…*): a continuous transmission or a burst with repeats is one long message; with *cut* on it is cut into frames starting at every occurrence of the *sync word*, or, when that field is empty, of a 32-bit word that repeats at a regular distance and is not a short-period pattern; the preamble before the word goes with the frame.
- **Analysing a part of the capture**: **Shift + drag** a span on the plot and press **analyse selection** — noise, centre and symbol rate are found in that span only (a rolling capture stops); **whole capture** goes back. Useful when a short packet is quieter than the noise around it. Dragging the noise line stops at the top of the plot and the vertical scale does not depend on it.
- **Spectrogram and the band filter** (*spectrogram above the plot*, on by default): a horizontal waterfall — time to the right on the same axis as the plot below (it zooms and pans with it), frequency up, absolute (the stream centre + offset; for a sound signal 0 … sr/2), colour is dBFS between the noise and the strongest line; *FFT size* (advanced) trades frequency against time resolution. It is computed in the worker from the whole capture. **Drag up / down on it** to pick a band (drag a band edge to resize, drag inside to move; a double click removes it); the tip shows the frequency under the cursor. The picked band goes to the outputs **`freq`** (its centre, absolute Hz), **`offset`** (from the stream centre) and **`bw`** (width, Hz) — wire `freq` to *IQ Frequency Shift* `freq` (the stream is moved to 0 Hz, the analyser then sees the signal in the middle) and `bw` on to the decimator or a filter; the outputs are empty without a band. The **band-pass filter** button (or *band-pass filter* in the node) analyses only the picked band, as URH's *apply bandpass filter* does: the band is moved to 0 Hz and low-passed in the frequency domain (−3 dB at the band edges, a cos roll-off 30 % of the width), then demodulated, so a strong neighbour no longer spoils the noise level and the symbol rate; the spectrogram keeps showing the whole capture. *raw* mode ignores the band. A change of the stream centre (a retuned receiver, or the shift fed back from `freq`) starts a new capture.
- **Outputs**: `blk` — the bits of each message as ±1 (feeds *CRC Finder*, *Conv Code Finder*, *Block Viewer*, *Viterbi*…); `rec` — a record per message (`hex`, `bin`, `text`, `n`, `baud`, `mod`, `start`, `dur`, and a field per label: the name with non-word characters replaced by `_`), so it goes straight to *HEX Field Finder*, *HEX Structure*, *Table* or the map; `bin` and `text` — the selected message; `baud`, `count`. In *rolling* mode a message is sent once, when it has left the edge of the window.
- **Limits**: a message that ends in zeros of an OOK code is cut where the signal drops below the noise (the trailing zero bits are lost); the symbol rate of a message of fewer than a dozen runs is uncertain; PSK has the usual phase ambiguity (the bits may come inverted, QPSK in any rotation — differential codes are not undone); there is no equalizer — use *CMA Equalizer* before the node on a multipath signal. It analyses what it captured: it does not transmit (the transmit chain is the *Symbol Player* → *RRC Pulse Shaper* → *FM Modulator* blocks).

What was checked: `node tools/test-siglab.mjs` — synthetic transmissions with a known content (preamble, sync word, length, payload, CRC-16) in OOK, 2FSK (1.2 – 38.4 kBd, carrier offsets, 12 dB SNR), 4FSK, BPSK, QPSK, Manchester over OOK and FSK, two rates in one capture, a continuous stream cut at the sync word: the modulation, the symbol rate (within 2 %), the number of messages and the bits (≥ 98.5 %) are compared with the source. In headless Chromium: the *IQ Generator* in ISM433 mode (OOK and FSK telegrams), rolling and single capture, zoom / selection / labels, the records reaching *HEX Field Finder*. `node tools/test-siglab.mjs` also checks the spectrogram (the tone's frequency and level, complex and real input) and the filter (gain in the band, rejection of the other band, −3 dB at the edge, a strong CW carrier next to an FSK transmission). Not tried on a real radio. Presets **Signal Analyzer: 433 MHz Remotes (Generator)** and **Signal Analyzer: Unknown Signal (Generator)**.

## Blind analysis

Nodes for taking apart a digital transmission whose parameters are not known. Each one prints what it found in its readout; wires carry the result on (a symbol rate to the filter and the slicer, frames to the next finder). The preset *Unknown Signal: Blind Analysis (Generator)* has the whole chain wired and a test transmitter in front of it.

`IQ → Baud Estimator → [CMA Equalizer] → FM Discriminator → RRC → Symbol Slicer → Sync Word Hunter → Conv Code Finder → CRC Finder → Block Viewer`

- **Baud Estimator** (`blindBaud`, Analysis, worker): the symbol rate of any IQ or real stream. Symbol edges give the spectrum of four features — the envelope, jumps of the envelope, jumps of the frequency, jumps of the complex sample — a line at the symbol rate (random NRZ data has no such line itself, the jumps do). Welch-averaged spectra, a peak search between *min* and *max*, harmonics folded to the fundamental (a line at f/2 or f/3 counts only when it is not weaker than −3 dB), the features vote. Outputs `baud` (held: it changes only after a shift of more than 0.3 %, so the slicer behind it is not restarted by every refinement; empty until a peak stands *thr* dB above the noise, so a wire from it leaves the downstream value alone), `conf` (dB), `sps`. Tested on 2FSK, GFSK, BPSK and QPSK from 1.2 to 19.2 kBd: the error is below 0.02 %.
- **CMA Equalizer** (`blindEq`, IQ, worker): a blind equalizer for constant-modulus signals (FSK, GMSK, PSK without a pulse shape, FM): y = Σ w·x, w ← w − μ·y(|y|² − 1)·x*. The echo of a multipath channel spoils the modulus of the envelope, the equalizer brings it back without knowing the data. The readout shows the dispersion of the modulus before → after (0.18 → 0.004 on 2FSK with a 0.35 echo). *taps* are at the stream rate (about 4 symbols × samples per symbol); *freeze* stops the adaptation.
- **Sync Word Hunter** (`syncHunt`, Protocols, worker): the input is the stream of symbols at the symbol rate (output of *Symbol Slicer*; *levels* 2 — a bit per symbol, 4 — a dibit). All words of *len* bits in the last *depth* bits are counted; candidates are the words that repeat at the same distance. Each candidate grows to the common run of all its repeats (preamble + word + a constant header), the alternating preamble is stripped (whole bytes) and the rest is the sync word; a run with a preamble wins, then the longest. The locked word stays while it is in the window. Frames of *period − word* bits (or *flen*) are cut after every match (*tol* bit errors) and go to `blk`; the frames of the window at the moment of the lock are replayed. `word` set by hand skips the search.
- **Conv Code Finder** (`convFind`, Protocols, worker): the frames of a convolutional code of rate 1/2 or 1/3, constraint length 3…9, polynomials in the usual octal notation (171, 133 for K=7). The streams of the outputs obey g_j·c_i = g_i·c_j whatever the data was, so the test does not need to know it: all pairs of polynomials are checked by that syndrome (about 0.1 s per pair of streams and bit phase, one such step per engine block). The true pair agrees over the whole message, a false one only over constant headers: the ranking is by the accumulated excess of agreement, then the shortest K (multiples of a pair agree as well; a wrong bit phase gives K + 1). The end of the coded part comes from the change point of that sum and, when the frame ends with K−1 zero bits (*tail*), from the minimum of the zero-state path metric. A candidate is accepted only if the Viterbi decoder leaves ≤ 7 % errors on real frames (uncoded text or random bits fail). Outputs `blk` — decoded frames — and `found`, `k`. An inverted output and bit errors (up to about 2 %) are fine; puncturing is not supported.
- **CRC Finder** (`crcFind`, Protocols, worker): the frames end with a checksum. A catalog of 51 CRC models (CRC-8, 16, 24, 32; every model is checked against the check value of "123456789") and four plain sums is tried with MSB- and LSB-first bytes, big- and little-endian, *maxSkip* header bytes the sum does not cover, optionally bit offsets 0–7, and up to *tail* extra bytes after the checksum (a pause, the next preamble). Not in the catalog: an unknown 8- or 16-bit polynomial is searched from frames of one length — a CRC is linear, so crc(a) ⊕ crc(b) = raw(a ⊕ b) whatever the init and xorout are; init and xorout are then given as the pair that fits that length. Outputs `blk` (frames without the checksum, only those that pass), `ok`, `found`.
- **Unknown Signal** (`blindGen`, IQ): a test transmission with known parameters: 2FSK, GFSK, BPSK or QPSK, preamble + sync word + message + checksum (a catalog CRC) + optional convolutional code (K=3, 5, 7, 9; rate 1/3 with K=7), random bits between frames, noise and an echo (gain, delay in symbols, phase). The `tx` output carries the frames as they were sent.

What was checked: every node against the generator and, for the finders, against synthetic frames with bit errors (the whole chain finds 9600 Bd 2FSK, B38D2E5A, K=7 (171, 133) and CRC-16/X-25, also with K=3/5/9, rate 1/3, GFSK, CRC-8/16/32 and without a code; the sync, code and CRC searches do not fire on random bits or uncoded text). **Not checked on a real recording.** No PSK demodulator is in the chain (the estimator works on PSK, the hunter takes symbols from a slicer); the finders search a code and a checksum by their algebra only — frames with burst errors, scramblers and interleavers have to be undone first.


### Measuring instruments

Meters with numeric outputs: a value can go to a *Number*, *Gauge*, *Compare* or a log. Presets: *Measure: Modulation Meter — FM and AM (Generator)*, *Measure: SINAD of an FM Receiver (Generator)*, *Measure: IQ Quality — DC and Imbalance (Generator)*.

- **Modulation Meter** (IQ, worker): a carrier near the center of the stream (shift it to zero and decimate first). The envelope gives **AM** — depth from the positive and negative peaks and their mean (a difference is overmodulation or distortion); the instantaneous frequency gives **FM** — peak deviation (half of the peak-to-peak), the modulating frequency (from zero crossings of the deviation) and the index β = deviation / modulating frequency. Also the carrier offset from the center and the level. Both signals pass two one-pole low-pass filters (*measurement band*), a window of 100…1000 ms; samples with the envelope below 10 % of the mean do not take part in FM. Outputs `am` (%), `dev`, `offset`, `fm`, `idx`, `level`. Needs complex IQ; with no carrier the outputs are empty. The modulating frequency is counted per window, so its resolution is 1 / window
- **SINAD / THD+N** (Analysis, signal): the standard receiver test. The tone is taken out by a least-squares sine fit (cos, sin and a constant) over the whole window, the frequency is found by a golden-section search around the FFT peak, so even 80 dB gets no leakage into the residual; what is left in the band is noise and distortion. **SINAD** = (S+N+D)/(N+D), **THD+N** = √((N+D)/S), also the tone frequency, its level, the residual level and ENOB. *Band*: 300–3400 Hz (voice, 4th-order filters), 20–20000 Hz or full; *tone* 0 = the strongest in the band, or a number / the `freq` input. Window 0.25…2 s, a result every half window. The FM receiver test is *IQ Demodulator → IQ → Audio → SINAD*. No psophometric weighting
- **IQ Quality** (IQ, worker): over a window of 250…2000 ms — the **DC offset** (dBFS and relative to the signal, dBc: LO leakage), the **Q gain** and **phase** error (the same model as *IQ Balance*: Q′ = g·(Q·cosφ + I·sinφ)) and the **image rejection**, from the circularity of the stream: ρ = |E[x²]| / E[|x|²] after removing the mean, rejection = 20·lg((1 + √(1 − ρ²)) / ρ). Works on a tone away from zero, noise or a busy band; a carrier exactly at the center (real envelope) reads too low, as does anything that is not symmetric by nature. Real streams are refused. Outputs `dc`, `dcc`, `gain`, `phase`, `irr`
- **Signal Meter** (IQ, worker): one signal in the stream — the strongest cluster of bins above the noise. Welch spectrum (Hann, 50 %, up to 64 frames); the noise floor is the 25th percentile of the central 90 % of bins (corrected for the averaged spectrum), the cluster is bins above *threshold* with gaps up to 1 % of the span merged. Outputs: `snr` (signal power minus noise, over the occupied bandwidth), `obw` (the band holding *share of power* — 99 % by default, equal tails), `bw` (width at −X dB from the peak, outermost crossings, so holes in an FM spectrum do not cut it short), `offset` / `freq` (from the center / absolute), `level` (signal, dBFS), `noise` (dBFS/Hz). A narrow signal (OBW ≤ 6 bins) is treated as a carrier: peak with a parabola on the log spectrum, then a fine estimate from the phase slope (shift to zero, boxcar, Σ x[k]·x*[k−1] over the window) — sub-hertz on a clean tone; a wide one gets the power centroid. Not a substitute for a calibrated analyzer: the noise floor assumes the signal fills less than ~75 % of the band, and the frequency is only as good as the SDR's clock (calibrate the PPM first)
- **Signal Database** (`sigDb`, worker): a lookup in the spirit of the Signal Identification Wiki, offline. Give any of *occupied bandwidth*, *symbol rate*, *FM deviation* and *frequency* (wire them from *Signal Meter* `obw` / `freq`, *Baud Estimator* `baud`, *Modulation Meter* `dev`, or type them) and it ranks about 80 known signals — analog voice and broadcast, HF digital modes, paging, aero / marine, digital voice and trunking, ISM / IoT, satellites, wideband (GSM, LTE, DVB, Wi-Fi, GPS…). Every given value scores 1 inside the signal's typical range and falls off with a Gaussian in octaves outside it (half an octave → 0.37); a feature the entry does not specify counts 0.85, a frequency outside the entry's bands ×0.2, so a specific match outranks a vague one. Outputs `name`, `mod`, `score` (0…1) and `text` (the ranked list). Several signals can tie at 100 % — DMR, P25, YSF and NXDN differ by little in these numbers, which is the honest answer. A hint, not a decoder; the table is `SDB` in `modules/sigdb-kernels.js` and easy to extend
- **PSK/QAM Receiver (EVM)** (`pskRx`, worker): an IQ stream of a BPSK / QPSK / 8PSK / 16QAM carrier (shifted near zero, ≥ 2 samples per symbol) → root-raised-cosine matched filter → symbol AGC → Gardner timing recovery with cubic interpolation → carrier recovery: a coarse offset from the M-th power of the symbols (for 16QAM only the corner points, whose 4th power is constant) while unlocked, then a second-order decision-directed loop. Against a constellation of unit average power it reports `evm` (%, RMS error over the last *EVM window* symbols divided by the RMS of the ideal points), `mer` (dB, −20·lg EVM), `foff` (carrier offset, Hz, as found by the loop — pull-in about ±2 % of the symbol rate, so shift and decimate first) and `lock`; the `out` wire carries the derotated symbols at the symbol rate for *Constellation*. Checked against a synthesized signal: QPSK at 10 / 20 dB SNR per sample (16 / 26 dB per symbol) gives EVM 15.9 / 5.1 % (theory 15.8 / 5.0), an offset of 1 % of the symbol rate is found to ±1 Hz. Not covered: differential and offset modulations (π/4-DQPSK, OQPSK), phase ambiguity is not resolved (EVM does not depend on it), 16QAM floor is about 1.8 % (interpolation and timing jitter)
- **Channel Activity** (`chanLog`, Analysis): a log of channels built from the labels of the *Signal Type Identifier* (`bands` in, same kind of `bands` out). Each analysis frame the labels are folded into persistent channels — a label joins a channel whose centre is within *tolerance* (or 0.6 × width) and whose width is within a factor of three, otherwise a new channel starts; centre and width are running means, the type is the most frequent label. **Occupancy** is the time a channel was seen divided by the time watched (the frame interval is credited to a channel seen in that frame; its first sighting adds nothing). Channels closer than *merge* (default 2.5 kHz) are shown as one — the sidebands of a tone-modulated FM and other fragments of one signal; 0 turns it off. The readout groups channels by width (narrow < 3 kHz, voice / data 3–16 kHz, wide 16–250 kHz, broadband > 250 kHz); *Save CSV* writes frequency, width, type, occupancy, hits, first and last seen, peak level; *Reset* starts a new watch. Outputs `bands` (occupancy in the label, for the spectrum analyzer), `text`, `count`, and `f` / `occ` of the busiest channel. The detector is `sigid`: where it splits one signal, the registry can only merge by distance
- **IQ Channel Model** (`iqChannel`, IQ, worker): a radio path between a transmitter and a receiver, for testing decoders and receivers without air. In order: sample clock error (*ppm*, cubic Lagrange interpolation, the stream gets slightly shorter or longer) → **multipath** (delay line; profiles *2-ray*, 3GPP *EPA / EVA / ETU* or a *custom* list `delay µs:gain dB, …`; tap power is normalised to 1; delays shorter than a sample period are linearly interpolated, so on a narrow stream they merge into flat fading) with **Rayleigh** or **Rician** (K-factor, first tap) fading — a sum of 24 sinusoids per tap with a given max Doppler spread *fd*, coefficients every sr/(16·fd) samples with linear interpolation, E|h|² = 1 → path gain → **Doppler** shift and drift in Hz/s (a satellite pass is about −50…−200) → **phase noise** (Wiener, oscillator linewidth in Hz) → I/Q imbalance and DC → impulse noise (bursts per second, length, level against the signal) and **AWGN**: *SNR* is taken against the signal power at the channel output (running mean ≈ 0.5 s), or an absolute level in dBFS. Outputs: `out`, `snr` (the SNR that is set, dB; empty with noise off) and `level` (signal level at the output, dBFS). The *seed* makes noise and fading repeatable. Preset: *Measure: Channel Model*. Tests: `node tools/test-kernels.mjs` (noise power, Doppler and drift, two-ray echo, Rayleigh / Rician statistics, ppm, phase-noise variance, impulse rate).
- **Spectrum Baseline** (`specBase`, Analysis; core `modules/baseline-kernels.js`): remembers what the band normally looks like and reports the difference — a watchman for hours of unattended work. The air is cut into cells (*cell width*, `auto` = the bin width on the 1-2-5 grid; a sweep or a retuned source just adds cells, the norm is kept). Each cell keeps its level and spread in dB (running mean and variance over visits) and how often a signal was there (*occ*). The first *learn* visits only teach. Then a cell is **above the norm** if it is higher by *thr* dB and by more than *z* σ (σ not less than *sdMin*); neighbouring cells join into an event, and an event wider than *wide* kHz is **wideband** (a jammer, a raised floor). A carrier that was present in ≥ *occMin* of the visits and is now at the noise level and *drop* dB below the norm is **missing**. An event is reported after *confirm* frames in a row and ends *hold* ms after it is gone. A deviation is absorbed into the norm slowly (1/20 of the normal rate), so a station that stays on becomes normal after about 2 × *adapt* visits. Outputs: `bands` (for the Spectrum Analyzer: orange — new, red — wideband, grey — missing), `rec` (`src:'BASELINE'`, `kind` new / wideband / missing, `status` start / end, `freq`, `bw`, `db`, `excess`, `floor`, `dur` on end — wire it to a Table for a log, or to MQTT / a webhook for alarms), `base` (the norm as a spectrum, to draw under the live one), `count`, `f` and `excess` of the strongest, `learned`. *learn only* updates the norm without reports. **Save norm / Load norm** keep it in a file (about 7 bytes per cell), so a night of learning is not lost. Preset: *Measure: Spectrum Baseline*. Tests: `node tools/test-baseline.mjs` (no false alarms on 300 stable frames, new signal on the 3rd frame, end with a duration, missing carrier, wideband rise, absorption of a permanent station, save / load, a two-part sweep).
- **Cross-Correlator (TDOA / interferometer)** (`iqXcorr`, Analysis, worker) and **IQ Delay** (`iqDelay`, IQ): the software half of a multi-receiver setup (two dongles on one antenna through a splitter, a common noise source, or two receivers of one signal). Two `iq` streams of the same rate are cut into blocks (by arrival order — the offset between the dongles is searched, ± *search range*) and cross-correlated. The integer lag is the peak of the averaged correlation power; the fractional part comes from the slope of the cross-spectrum phase, refined coarse-to-fine (phase differences over 1, 8, 64, 512 bins, each after removing the delay already found), which is insensitive to the random phase of each block caused by the two local oscillators and is close to the theoretical accuracy: 0.0006 samples at high SNR, 0.03 at 0 dB per channel on a wideband source. Outputs: `delay` (samples, b − a: positive when b lags), `dist` (path difference, m), `bearing` (° — asin of the path difference over the *antenna baseline*, with a baseline set), `phase` (what is left after the delay is removed — the carrier phase difference of a narrowband signal, for interferometry; meaningful only with a shared reference clock), `coh` (normalised correlation at the peak, ≈ SNR/(1+SNR) for equal noise) and `drift` (the sample-clock difference between the two receivers, ppm, from the trend of the delay). **Zero** copies the current delay and phase into *delay zero* / *phase zero*: feed both receivers from a common noise source first, so cable lengths and receiver latency drop out. *IQ Delay* delays a stream by a fractional number of samples with a phase shift (windowed sinc, reliable up to ~0.4 of the sample rate) for calibration and tests; the filter is causal, so it has a fixed latency of 16 samples on top of the delay — pass the other stream through an *IQ Delay* with 0 to compare. What this does not do: the sampling of two independent dongles is not synchronised in hardware, so a bearing from the phase needs a shared clock (or a re-calibration every few minutes), and the node does not drive the receivers — it works on whatever two `iq` wires it is given

### Logic Analyzer

**Logic Analyzer** (Analysis): 1…8 channels → digital traces on a common time axis, with a decoder that gives out the **bits and bytes**.

- **Input wires**: *signal* (audio rate — the usual choice, e.g. the `bit` output of *Transmit Chars*, a demodulator's soft output, a square wave) or *number* (one sample per engine block, ~Eng.sr / block, for slow things: lamps, triggers, flip-flops, Compare outputs). A *threshold* with *hysteresis* turns each wire into 0 / 1. The `d1…dN` outputs are the digitised channels. At 44.1 kHz async serial works up to ~9600 baud (at least 4 samples per bit) and I²C / SPI clocks up to ~10 kHz; the readout warns when the baud is too high for the sample rate.
- **History**: drag the traces sideways to scroll back through the last 20 s (the view holds), the wheel changes the window, a double click returns to the live trace; *Hold / Run* still freezes the picture.
- **Display**: the *window* (1 ms … 10 s) is drawn from the list of edges, not samples, so even long windows are cheap. *Trigger* on a rising / falling edge of a chosen channel (the trigger sits at 25 % of the window; the time axis is relative to it), *single shot* with **Arm**, **Hold / Run** freezes the picture. Decoded bytes are drawn as bubbles on the trace (hex and the character, errors in red).
- **Decoders** (*decoder*; channels are set in the advanced parameters):
  - **UART**: baud, 5…16 data bits, parity (none / even / odd), idle level, bit order (LSB first by default). Start bit is checked in the middle, every bit is sampled at its centre; framing and parity errors are marked and not sent to `text`.
  - **SPI**: clock, MOSI, MISO (optional), CS (optional, active low), word size, order (MSB first by default), sampling edge (rising = modes 0 / 3, falling = modes 1 / 2).
  - **I²C**: SCL + SDA; START / repeated START / STOP, 7-bit address with R / W, data bytes, ACK / NAK (a NAK is marked with `!`).
- **Outputs**: `byte` (last byte), `new` (pulse in the block where a byte arrived — wire it to a lamp or a counter), `text` (a line: UART — on a line feed, after 80 characters or after 1 s of silence; SPI — `MOSI … | MISO …` after CS goes high; I²C — `S 3C+W 00 AF P` after STOP), `rec` (a record per byte: `proto`, `byte`, `hex`, `ch`, `time` — seconds from the start, `err`; I²C adds `kind` and `addr`, SPI `line`), `hit` (a pulse on every trigger). `text` goes to a *Text Ticker*, `rec` to a *Rec Log*.

- **USB logic analyzers** (WebUSB; `input` → *USB* in the advanced parameters): cheap 8-channel analyzers on the Cypress FX2 (Saleae clones, boards with sigrok **fx2lafw** firmware). Channels 1…8 are D0…D7, 8-bit samples at 20 kHz … 24 MS/s straight into the same traces, trigger and UART / SPI / I²C decoders (the `ch` wires disappear). Buttons: *USB: connect* (opens the chooser), *USB: load firmware (.fw)*, *USB: start*, *USB: stop*. A board without firmware (it does not answer the version request) needs a fx2lafw image: take it from the `sigrok-firmware-fx2lafw` package (`/usr/share/sigrok-firmware/`) or from PulseView (`fx2lafw-saleae-logic.fw` for Saleae clones, `fx2lafw-cypress-fx2.fw` for a bare CY7C68013A board) — it is GPL-2.0+, so it is not bundled; it is loaded into the board's RAM and lives until the power is cut. The board then restarts; connect again. The page keeps up with a few MS/s: the readout shows the rate and the captured samples and warns when samples are dropped. Chrome / Edge / Opera only; on Linux the device needs a udev rule (or `chmod`) for the browser to open it.

Presets: *Logic Analyzer: UART Decode*, *Logic Analyzer: USB (fx2lafw)*.
